No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a door log.

'The Charge Cage outer door,' she said. 'It's supposed to interlock with the test sequence. When a run is armed, the door locks. Nobody in, nobody out, until the system stands down.' She looked at Marcus Rourke. 'At 09:22, the door unlocked. Mid-sequence.'

'Doors get overridden,' Rourke said. 'For access. It's a function. It exists for a reason.'

'It does,' Ellery said. 'Maintenance access. Emergency egress. Legitimate reasons.' She read from the log. 'The override at 09:22 was issued from your relay panel. No work order attached. No emergency logged.'

Rourke was quiet.

'Michael Holloway walked through that door at 09:24,' she said. 'Two minutes after you unlocked it. Into an armed test bay. The interlock that should have stopped him at the threshold—you'd already opened it.'

'I don't track who walks through doors,' Rourke said. 'I open them. People go where they go. That's not on me.'

'Did Michael ask you to unlock it?'

The room went still.

Rourke didn't answer.

'That's the question, isn't it,' Ellery said. 'Whether he asked. Because if he asked, then he walked into that bay knowing the interlock was down, and that changes the whole shape of this. And if he didn't ask—then you opened a locked safety door, mid-test, for no logged reason, two minutes before he died.' She leaned back. 'I can't tell which is worse for you. Neither can you. That's why you're not answering.'

'He was my friend,' Rourke said. It came out flat, like a fact he was reading off a sheet. 'From back home. Thirty years.'

'I know,' Ellery said. 'I think that's exactly why he could ask you for something he shouldn't, and you'd do it. A stranger asks you to override a safety interlock, you say no. A friend of thirty years asks—' she stopped. 'You open the door. Routine.'

Rourke looked at his hands.

'The report,' she said, 'will note that the Charge Cage interlock was overridden mid-sequence at 09:22. It will note the source. It will note the absence of any work order or emergency.'

She gathered her file.

'You did your job,' she said. 'That's what you'll tell yourself. You opened a door because someone with the right to ask, asked. The trouble is the interlock was the thing keeping him alive, and you're the one who turned it off, and you did it because you couldn't say no to him.'

She left.

The log stayed open. 09:22. Door unlocked. Source: relay panel one.
