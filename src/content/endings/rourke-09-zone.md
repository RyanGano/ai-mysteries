No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a map of the building's alarm zones.

'This whole wing is one zone,' she said. 'When a test arms, the zone arms with it. Motion, presence, the works. Except—' she pointed. 'You carved the Charge Cage out. Made it its own sub-zone. And then you disarmed the sub-zone for the morning. "Equipment staging."'

'We were staging equipment,' Rourke said. 'A pallet of cells came in. You can't move a pallet through an armed motion zone. It throws alarms the whole way. So you disarm the zone you're working in. That's standard.'

'When did the staging finish?'

A pause. '08:40. Around there.'

'And when did you re-arm the zone?'

The room went still.

Rourke didn't answer.

'You didn't,' Ellery said. 'The pallet was in by 08:40. The test armed at 09:30. For fifty minutes, the rest of the building thought the Charge Cage was protected, because the master board said the wing was armed. But the sub-zone you'd carved out was dark. And you never turned it back on.'

'It's a step,' Rourke said. 'Re-arming after staging. It's a step you do at the end. I must have—there was a lot going on that morning.'

'There was,' Ellery said. 'I believe that. I don't think you left it dark on purpose. I think you carved out the sub-zone for a real reason, and disarmed it for a real reason, and then the morning got busy and you forgot the last step, the way anyone forgets the last step.' She paused. 'But you're the one who built the carve-out. Nobody else knew the Cage was its own zone. So nobody else knew it needed re-arming. You created a hole that only you could see, and then you were the one who didn't close it.'

Rourke looked at the map.

'You made it efficient,' Ellery said. 'A separate zone so staging wouldn't disrupt the whole wing. Smart. Reasonable. And it meant the protection on that one room depended entirely on you remembering one switch. And on the one morning it mattered, you didn't.'

'It would have caught him,' Rourke said quietly. 'If it'd been armed. The motion sensor. It would have caught him walking in.'

'Yes,' Ellery said. 'It would have.'

The room was silent.

'The report,' she said, 'will note that the Charge Cage was configured as an independent alarm sub-zone, that the sub-zone was disarmed for equipment staging, and that it was not re-armed prior to the test.'

She rolled up the map.

'You built a smarter system,' she said. 'And the smarter system had one more place for a man to forget. And the man was you.'

She left.
