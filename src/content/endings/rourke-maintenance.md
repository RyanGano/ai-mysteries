No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery set a printout on the table.

'Access log,' she said. 'Charge Cage. The morning of the incident.'

Marcus Rourke looked at it. He had the stillness of someone who'd learned, a long time ago, that the best thing to do when confronted with something uncomfortable was nothing.

'Maintenance mode,' she said. 'Presence-detection layer. Toggled at 08:51. Cleared at 09:47.'

'Routine,' he said.

'That's the word in the log, yes.' She sat down. 'You wrote it.'

He said nothing.

'Maintenance mode is a legitimate tool,' she said. 'It's used for calibration runs, for equipment staging, for scenarios where human presence is guaranteed to be absent.' She paused. 'The test was scheduled for 09:30.'

'Maintenance windows sometimes overlap scheduled work.'

'They do,' she said. 'And when they do, there's a handoff protocol. A confirmation. Someone signs off that the area is clear before mode is restored.' She looked at the log. 'There was no handoff.'

Rourke's jaw tightened, almost imperceptibly.

'Michael Holloway walked into the Charge Cage at 09:24,' Ellery said. 'The presence-detection layer didn't register him. Because it wasn't looking.' She leaned back. 'You'd worked with Michael for a long time.'

'Since the beginning.'

'And you knew the schedule.'

'I knew a lot of schedules. That's the job.'

'He'd asked you for something,' she said. 'A few weeks before. Something you hadn't been able to give him. Or hadn't wanted to.' She watched him. 'And something you'd told him would stay between the two of you.'

Rourke looked at the table.

'I'm not saying you planned it,' she said. 'I'm saying you made a decision—a procedural decision, the kind you make a dozen times a week, the kind that disappears into the log as "routine"—and you made it on a morning when the consequences of that decision were not routine.'

The room held its breath.

'The report,' she said, 'will note that the maintenance-mode toggle occurred within authorized parameters. It will also note that no handoff confirmation was logged. It will also note that the person who toggled it knew Michael Holloway's schedule, knew the suit's history, and knew—' she stopped. 'You knew what he was carrying.'

Rourke didn't deny it.

'That's not how it works,' he said finally. His voice was flat. 'You can't charge someone for a maintenance flag.'

'I know,' Ellery said. 'That's the thing about doing one small thing correctly. It means the record is clean.' She picked up the printout. 'The record is very clean.'

She folded it and put it in her file.

'I think you've been cleaning things up for a long time,' she said. 'I think that's the job you actually do.'

She left.

Rourke looked at his hands for a moment. Then he picked up his radio and clipped it back on his belt and walked out after her.

His footsteps were even. They were always even.
