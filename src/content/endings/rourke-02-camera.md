No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a timeline of footage.

'Charge Cage camera,' she said. 'Continuous recording. Except here.' She pointed at a gap. 'Eighteen minutes. Right across the incident. The log says "scheduled storage maintenance."'

Marcus Rourke looked at it without expression.

'Storage cycles,' he said. 'They run. It's routine.'

'They run at 02:00,' Ellery said. 'Every camera in the building. This one ran at 09:00. By itself. Just this camera, just this morning, just this window.'

Rourke said nothing.

'I'm not a technical person,' she said. 'You know that. I ask the stupid questions. So here's one. Can a storage cycle be triggered manually?'

'It can.'

'By whom?'

'Anyone with the camera console.'

'Who has the camera console?'

A pause. 'Security.'

'How many people in Security?'

The pause was longer this time. 'On that shift,' he said, 'one.'

The room was very quiet.

'I'm not saying you made the cameras lie,' Ellery said. 'A gap isn't a lie. A gap is just—absence. Nothing's recorded. There's nothing to contradict. That's cleaner than a lie. A lie can be caught. A gap can only be noticed.' She paused. 'I noticed.'

'Cameras fail,' Rourke said. 'Footage gets lost. It happens. It's not evidence of anything except that the system isn't perfect.'

'It happens,' she agreed. 'It happened to exactly the camera that was pointed at the exact place a man died, in the exact window he died in. The system isn't perfect, and it failed in the single most convenient way it possibly could.' She closed the timeline. 'That's a lot of luck, Marcus. For a man who doesn't believe in luck.'

He looked at the table.

'The report,' she said, 'will note that the Charge Cage footage for the incident window is unavailable due to a manually-initiated storage cycle. It will note the time. It will note that the cycle was non-standard and initiated from the security console.'

She stood.

'You downplay things with paperwork,' she said. 'Storage maintenance. Routine. I've watched you do it three times now. You don't deny. You reclassify.' She paused at the door. 'There's no footage of whatever you're protecting. But there's footage of you deciding there shouldn't be.'

She left.

The gap stayed in the timeline, eighteen minutes of nothing, the cleanest thing in the building.
