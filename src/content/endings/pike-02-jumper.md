No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery set a small object on the table. A jumper. A short loop of wire with a clip on each end.

'You know what this is,' she said.

Jonah Pike looked at it and nodded fast. 'It's a bypass jumper. You use it to—when you're testing a circuit, you bridge across the interlock so it doesn't keep tripping while you work. Then you take it out.'

'When did you take this one out?'

The room went quiet.

'I—' Jonah looked at the table. 'I put it in to do the grounding test. The interlock kept cutting power and I couldn't get a clean reading with it cutting in and out. So I jumped it. Just to finish the test.'

'And then?'

'And then I meant to pull it,' he said. The words came faster. 'I always pull it. It's the first thing, you pull the jumper before you button up. I know that. Everybody knows that. I've done it a thousand—' He stopped.

'It was still in the suit,' Ellery said. 'When Michael Holloway put it on. The interlock was bridged. The whole time. It couldn't trip because you'd taught it not to.'

Jonah's hands were shaking a little. He put them under the table.

'Priya flagged the window,' he said, very quietly. 'Forty minutes for ninety minutes of work. I was going so fast. I was watching the clock the whole time. And when I buttoned up I was already thinking about the next thing, the next—and I just—' He couldn't finish.

'You forgot the jumper,' Ellery said.

'I forgot the jumper.'

The room was silent.

'I'm not going to tell you it was complicated,' Ellery said. 'It wasn't. It was a piece of wire you leave in for two minutes and take out. That's all it ever was. You've done it right a thousand times. This time you didn't.' She paused. 'I think you'd give anything to make it complicated. To make it somebody's policy or somebody's design. But it was a jumper, and it was yours, and it was in.'

'It passed,' Jonah said. It came out small. 'The test passed. With the jumper in, everything reads fine. That's the whole problem. It reads fine. It always reads fine, because the thing that's supposed to say no is the thing you turned off.'

'Yes,' Ellery said. 'That's the whole problem.'

She looked at him for a long moment, and there was something almost gentle in it.

'The report,' she said, 'will note that a bypass jumper was found installed across the interlock at the time of the incident. It will note its purpose. It will note that it was not removed after the maintenance procedure.'

She didn't pick the jumper up. She left it sitting there, small and harmless, on the table between them.

'You answer fast when you're scared,' she said. 'I noticed it the first day. You worked fast that morning too. I think those are the same thing.'

She left.

The jumper stayed where it was.
