No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a term sheet.

'A side agreement,' she said. 'With a strategic customer. Signed by you, four months ago. It commits a delivery date.' She slid it across. 'The date is eight weeks before the science team says the suit will be ready.'

Priya Shah glanced at it. 'That's a standard commercial document. Aggressive timelines are how deals get signed. Everyone in the industry pads, and everyone knows everyone pads.'

'Did Michael know about this one?'

The room went quiet.

'Michael had veto power,' Ellery said. 'And Michael was getting cautious. Funding was tight, a safety incident would sink it, and he'd started slowing things down. A side deal with a hard date he hadn't approved—if he saw it, he kills it. And if he kills it, the customer walks, and the gap between what you promised and what exists becomes very public, very fast.'

'You're building a motive out of a calendar,' Priya said. 'The reality is that every executive in this building has commitments that are ahead of engineering. That's the job. You sell the future. Engineering catches up.'

'And what happens,' Ellery said, 'when the man with the veto is the only thing standing between you and the day engineering doesn't catch up?'

Priya didn't answer.

'I'm not saying you planned anything,' Ellery said. 'I want to be clear about that. I'm saying you had a deadline that only existed because you'd hidden it, and the one person who could expose it was Michael, and that morning's test was the result that would have let you keep hiding it a little longer. So you pushed the test through. Every decision you made that day points the same direction—and the direction is the date on this page.'

'The reality is—' Priya started.

'The reality,' Ellery said, 'is that you signed a promise the science couldn't keep, and then you spent four months arranging the world so the promise wouldn't be caught. The test was part of that arrangement. Michael being there was a risk to it. And now Michael isn't a risk to anything.'

The room was very silent.

'I can't prove you wanted him gone,' Ellery said. 'I can prove you needed the test to run, and I can prove why, and I can prove you knew the suit wasn't ready for it. That's not nothing. That's most of it.'

She tapped the term sheet.

'The report will note an undisclosed delivery commitment inconsistent with the engineering timeline. It will note that its disclosure would have triggered the founder's veto. It will note who signed it, and who didn't know.'

She stood.

'You said it yourself once,' she said. '"At some point you have to make a call." You made a lot of them. They all bought you time. He paid for the time. Not you.'

She left the term sheet on the table.
