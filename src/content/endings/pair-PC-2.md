No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a grounding repair and a widened threshold.

'Jonah,' she said, 'you repaired the grounding mesh with non-spec material. It conducts, it passes continuity, but it doesn't carry the load when the load is real. A fault would put current somewhere it shouldn't go.' She turned. 'Renée, you'd widened the monitoring threshold that watches for exactly that—current where it shouldn't be. You raised the bar to cut false alarms.' She set the records down. 'You made the leak. You raised the line that would have caught it.'

Jonah stared at the photo of the mesh. Renée was still.

'Here's the sequence,' Ellery said. 'The substitute mesh let stray current build past where the spec mesh would have shunted it. That building current threw a reading—a small one at first, growing. The monitoring system watches for that. It would have escalated, early, with room to spare.' She looked at Renée. 'Except you'd moved the threshold out. The early reading fell under your new line. By the time it crossed, it was too late.'

'I was out of the right mesh,' Jonah said. 'It looked the same. I didn't know it would—'

'And I retuned the alerting to make it usable,' Renée said. 'The false-positive rate was unmanageable.'

'Both reasonable,' Ellery said. 'Jonah, you made do with what you had, under a deadline, on a part that looked identical. Renée, you tuned out the noise so the real alarms would be trusted. And the consequence of your two reasonable choices is this: a slow leak that the monitoring was specifically designed to catch, fitted exactly into the blind spot your retuning had opened.'

The room was very quiet.

'If the mesh had been spec,' Ellery said, 'there's no leak, and your threshold never matters, Renée. If your threshold had stayed where it was, Jonah's leak gets caught early, and the bad mesh becomes a near-miss instead of a death. Each of your mistakes was harmless without the other. The bad part needed the blind monitor. The blind monitor needed the bad part. You supplied each other's missing piece.'

'I never saw his repair,' Renée said. 'I had no way to know the mesh was substituted.'

'And I never touched the monitoring,' Jonah said. 'I didn't even know there was a threshold to widen.'

'No,' Ellery said. 'You worked in different layers. He works in the metal. You work in the math. And the metal sprang a leak the math was tuned not to see. Two people, two layers, one current finding the one path that neither of you was watching.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the non-specification grounding repair and the widened detection threshold, that the leak fell below the threshold, and that each failure was survivable without the other.'

She stood.

'He made a leak. You looked away from where it would show,' she said. 'Not on purpose. Not together. But the current found the seam between your two reasonable mistakes, and it ran straight through a man.'

She left the photo and the configuration on the table.
