No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery drew a line on the whiteboard. A single horizontal line, left to right.

'I'm going to walk it forward,' she said. 'The morning, in order. Not who's guilty. Just what happened, one thing handing off to the next.'

She made a mark at the left end.

'06:01. Priya flags the test must-run. The go/no-go review is foreclosed before anyone arrives.' A mark. '07:14. Marcus's maintenance flag suppresses the proximity layer. Nobody will be warned at the door.' A mark. '08:40. The recalibration Elias deferred means the sensors are running drifted.' A mark. '08:50. Jonah, with forty minutes for ninety minutes of work, skips the second verification. The suit reads normal because the thing that would say otherwise is bypassed.' A mark. '09:02. The anomalous reading arrives. It falls under the alert threshold Renée widened. Nothing escalates.'

She'd reached the middle of the line.

'09:24. Michael walks into the bay. The door doesn't stop him—Marcus's override. The presence layer doesn't see him—the suppressed flag. The system doesn't halt—the readings are within the drifted, narrowed, unverified envelope everyone helped set.' A mark near the right end. '09:30. The discharge.'

She capped the marker.

'Look at the line,' she said. 'Every mark is a handoff. Every handoff is one person's decision becoming the next person's normal. Priya's flag became the condition Jonah worked under. Jonah's skipped check became the clean reading the system trusted. Elias's drift became the band the reading fell inside. Marcus's flag became the silence at the door. Renée's threshold became the reason the alarm stayed quiet.' She paused. 'Pull any one mark off the line, and the chain breaks, and he lives. Any one.'

The room was silent.

'That's the thing about a chain,' Ellery said. 'No link thinks it's the chain. Each link is just a link. Priya didn't skip a check—Jonah did. Jonah didn't widen a threshold—Renée did. Renée didn't suppress a sensor—Marcus did. Every one of you can point to the link before you and the link after, and say, that wasn't me.' She looked at them. 'And you'd all be right. And the man is still dead, because a chain doesn't need any single link to be the cause. It just needs all of them to hold.'

No one spoke.

'I can't break this in court,' she said. 'I want to be honest with you about that. A chain where every link is individually defensible is the hardest thing in the world to prosecute, because the law looks for the cause, and there isn't one. There's a sequence. Sequences don't go to prison.'

She looked at the line on the board, end to end.

'The report,' she said, 'will reproduce this timeline. Every mark, every handoff, every name. Laid out in order, the way it actually happened. So that anyone who reads it can see what none of you could see from inside your own link: the whole chain, holding, all the way to 09:30.'

She left the line on the board.

It ran the full width of the wall, unbroken.
