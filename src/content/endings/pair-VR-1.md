No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had two findings, and she set them down one beside the other.

'Two questions guard that bay,' she said. 'Is the suit dangerous? And is anyone there? Two separate systems, two separate answers. Both have to fail for a man to die.' She looked at Elias Varga, then at Marcus Rourke. 'One of you broke each one.'

Neither spoke.

'Elias,' she said. 'You narrowed the discharge threshold. The system that asks "is this dangerous" was tuned to say no to a wider range of faults—including the one that killed him.' She turned. 'Marcus, you suppressed the proximity layer. The system that asks "is anyone there" was told no one was. The discharge was answered by your threshold. The man was answered by your flag.'

'Those are unrelated changes,' Varga said. 'Made independently, weeks apart, for separate and legitimate reasons. There's no connection between a calibration decision and a security flag.'

'That's exactly the point,' Ellery said. 'There's no connection. You never spoke. You didn't coordinate. You each adjusted your own system for your own reason. Elias, you widened what counts as safe. Marcus, you blinded the room to the man. Alone, either is survivable—a dangerous fault still gets caught if the system sees the person; a person in the bay still gets protected if the fault trips the halt.' She paused. 'It took both. The fault that didn't trip, in the bay that couldn't see him.'

The room was very still.

'I keep looking for the meeting where you planned this,' Ellery said. 'There isn't one. There's a scientist who trusts his numbers and a security man who trusts his routines, and they each removed one of the two things keeping a man alive, on the same morning, without ever knowing the other had done it.'

'You can't hold me responsible for what he did to the sensors,' Rourke said. 'I don't even understand what he did to the sensors.'

'No,' Ellery said. 'And he doesn't understand your flag. That's your shared defense, and it's airtight, and it's why he's dead. Each of you removed one guardrail, certain the other was still up. Neither of you checked. Why would you? It wasn't your guardrail.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the narrowed threshold and the suppressed presence layer, that they originated independently, and that the death required both. It will name you both. It will not connect you, because nothing connects you except the morning and the body.'

She gathered the two pages.

'You're strangers, in a way,' she said. 'You barely work together. And between the two of you, with no plan and no contact, you took down both walls of the same room.'

She left.
