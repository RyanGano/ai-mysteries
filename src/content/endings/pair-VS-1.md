No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a calibration log and a calendar.

'Elias,' she said, 'you deferred the recalibration. The sensors ran drifted. You had a technical reason and the procedure permitted it.' She turned. 'Priya, you compressed the schedule and flagged the test must-run. There was no time to question the drift and no authority to pause for it.' She set the two records side by side. 'Drifted sensors, and a schedule that wouldn't allow anyone to notice.'

'Those are separate decisions,' Priya said. 'Made by separate people in separate functions. I don't manage calibration. Elias doesn't manage the schedule.'

'No,' Ellery said. 'You don't. That's what makes this work. Elias's deferral was survivable—drifted sensors get caught at the next checkpoint, normally. Except there was no next checkpoint, because Priya's schedule had removed it and her must-run flag had foreclosed the review where someone would have asked about the drift.' She paused. 'His decision created the hazard. Yours removed the moment anyone could have caught it.'

The room was quiet.

'There's a history between you,' Ellery said. 'Science and shipping. You've fought for two years—him saying it's too fast, you saying it has to move. This is that fight, except this time you both won. He won the right to defer his maintenance for technical reasons. You won the right to hold the schedule. And the place where your two victories met was a suit running on bad calibration with no one allowed to stop and check.'

'I deferred a maintenance task by a week,' Varga said. 'Within the manual. The manual I wrote.'

'And I set a schedule within my authority,' Priya said.

'You did,' Ellery agreed. 'Both true. And each of you, defending your own decision, points at the other's as the real problem. Elias: the drift would've been caught if Priya hadn't killed the review. Priya: the schedule would've been fine if Elias hadn't run drifted sensors. You're both right. That's the trap. Each of your decisions was safe in a world where the other person had made the opposite choice.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the deferred recalibration and the compressed, must-run schedule. It will note that the drift was the hazard and the schedule was the reason no one caught it. It will name you both.'

She looked between them, the scientist and the operator, two years of argument finally agreeing on something.

'You spent two years on opposite sides,' she said. 'It turns out opposite sides is exactly the configuration that kills a man. He builds the danger slowly and carefully. You make sure no one has time to look. You needed each other for this. You just never knew you were working together.'

She left the log and the calendar on the table, touching at one corner.
