No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a near-miss log.

'Three weeks before the incident,' she said, 'Suit Seven discharged during an unmanned run. The same fault. Empty bay, no one hurt. It generated a near-miss entry. The entry routes to you—near-misses are safety's to assess.' She looked at Renée Calder. 'You assessed it. You closed it.'

'I assessed it as a contained, no-harm event with no recurrence trigger identified,' Renée said. 'That's the correct disposition for an unmanned anomaly with no root cause established. You log it, you flag it for trend analysis, you close the immediate item. I followed the standard.'

'You established no root cause,' Ellery said. 'That's the part. The fault fired and you couldn't find why, and instead of holding the program until you could, you closed it as "no recurrence trigger identified." But you hadn't identified anything. You'd just failed to. Those aren't the same.'

'An open near-miss with no root cause blocks every run indefinitely,' Renée said. 'You can't find a cause for everything. At some point you make a judgment that the event was anomalous and you resume, with monitoring. If safety blocked all operations until every anomaly was fully explained, this lab would have shut down years ago. I made the call any safety professional makes.'

'You're the one who taught me the phrase,' Ellery said. '"This is how it starts." You've said it to half the people in this building. An unexplained fault, contained, closed quick, everyone relieved—and then the same fault, three weeks later, not contained. That's not a freak sequence. That's the sequence you've spent your career warning everyone about. And you closed the first one yourself.'

Renée was silent.

'Why did you close it fast?' Ellery asked. 'You, of all people. You never close things fast.'

The room was very quiet.

'Because I was tired,' Renée said finally. The first unguarded thing. 'Because I had four open investigations and a stop-work memo on another line and a board that treated every one of my holds as obstruction. Because keeping it open meant a fight I didn't have the strength for that week. So I told myself it was anomalous, and I closed it, and I felt the wrongness of it and I closed it anyway.' She looked up. 'I knew better. That's the part you want. I knew better and I was just so tired of knowing better and being alone in it.'

'I'm not enjoying this,' Ellery said quietly. 'I want you to understand that. You're the one in this building who was usually right. That makes the one time you talked yourself out of it harder, not easier.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note that the prior unmanned discharge generated a near-miss assessment, that the assessment closed the item without establishing root cause, and that the disposition was made by you.'

She stood.

'You spent fifteen years being the person who didn't look away,' she said. 'You earned the right to be tired. You just didn't get to be tired on the week the warning was real.'

She left the near-miss log on the table, closed, no root cause, three weeks early.
