No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had an incident file. An old one.

'Four years ago,' she said. 'A near-miss in the original test bay. Someone was hurt. Not badly. It was reported up the chain and then—' she turned a page. 'It wasn't. The report exists. It was never filed externally. The regulator never saw it.'

Marcus Rourke went very still.

'Your signature's on the internal version,' she said. 'As the security officer of record. And then the external filing, the one that's legally required, never happened. Someone made it go away.' She looked at him. 'Michael Holloway's name is on the thank-you note.'

'That was a long time ago,' Rourke said.

'It was,' Ellery agreed. 'And it's been sitting between the two of you ever since. A thing you did for him. A thing that, if it came out, ends his funding and your career, both. The kind of thing that ties two people together so tight that neither one can ever fully trust the other again.' She paused. 'How was Michael using it, Marcus?'

He didn't answer.

'Here's what I think,' she said. 'I think Michael was the kind of man who kept score. I think a favor like that doesn't stay a favor. It becomes leverage. I think he'd started asking you for things—small things, then bigger things—and reminding you, without quite saying it, what you both had to lose.'

'He wouldn't have,' Rourke said. But it came out without conviction.

'And I think,' Ellery went on, 'that the morning of the incident, there was a safety step you should have enforced and didn't. A door, a sensor, a sign-off. Something. And you let it slide. Because the man who could destroy you asked you to, and you'd been letting things slide for him for four years, and one more didn't feel like the one that mattered.'

The room was silent.

'I can't prove the conversation,' Ellery said. 'There's no recording of a man leaning on his oldest friend. That kind of thing doesn't go in a log. But the cover-up's in writing. The favor's in writing. And the favor explains everything that isn't.'

Rourke looked older than he had when she walked in.

'The report,' she said, 'will note a prior unreported incident, and the security officer who knew of its suppression. It will not speculate about leverage. It will only lay the two things side by side and let whoever reads it draw the line between them.'

She stood.

'You covered for him once,' she said. 'And it cost you the rest of your life saying yes. I think you'd have paid almost anything to make that first lie not exist. But the only person who could've forgiven it is the one you couldn't save.'

She left the old file on the table, next to the new one.
