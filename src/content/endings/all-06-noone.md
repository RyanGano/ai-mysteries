No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery sat down and was quiet for a while before she spoke.

'I came here looking for a person,' she said. 'That's what I do. Someone solves a case by finding the one who did it. I've done it my whole career. You find the thread, you pull it, it leads to a hand. There's always a hand.' She looked around the table. 'There's no hand here. I've looked. There isn't one.'

No one spoke.

'I kept thinking I was failing,' she said. 'Weeks of it. Every lead led to a person who'd done one small, reasonable, defensible thing. I'd think, this is it, this is the one—and then I'd see the thing in context and it would dissolve. A flag. A clock. A check. A threshold. A memo. Each one innocent. I thought I just wasn't good enough to find the guilty one.' She paused. 'And then I understood. I wasn't failing to find the guilty one. There isn't a guilty one. That's not the failure. That's the answer.'

'Then we can all go home,' Priya said.

'You can,' Ellery said. 'That's the worst part. You can all go home, and every one of you will be telling the truth when you say you didn't do it. Marcus didn't do it. Elias didn't do it. Jonah didn't do it. Renée didn't do it. You didn't do it, Priya. Not one of you did it.' She let it sit. 'And Michael Holloway is dead, and you all did it, and both of those things are completely true at the same time, and I have spent my whole life believing that was impossible.'

The room was very still.

'I solve cases with human psychology and timeline discipline,' Ellery said. 'That's my reputation. But those tools assume an actor. A motive that becomes a deed. I can trace a deed back to a person every time.' She shook her head slowly. 'I can't trace this back to a person, because it didn't come from a person. It came from the shape you all made when you stood next to each other. The deed has no author. It has a configuration.'

'You can't charge a configuration,' Elias said. Quietly, for once. Not a correction. An observation.

'No,' Ellery said. 'You can't. That's exactly the problem, and you've put your finger on it, and I think you've known it from the start. There's no one to charge. The law needs a who. This has a how and a what and a when and five names that each only touch the edge of it. It doesn't have a who.' She paused. 'I think that terrifies me more than a murderer would. A murderer I understand. A murderer means the world still works the way I thought. This means a man can die and the universe can decline to provide anyone responsible.'

No one argued.

'The report,' she said, 'will be the first I've ever written without a conclusion. It will describe everything that happened, completely and accurately. And where the line for "responsible party" goes, it will say: no single individual. It will be the truest thing I've ever written and it will feel like the biggest lie.'

She stood.

'Go home,' she said. 'All of you. You didn't do it.' She picked up her bag. 'I'll never sleep again, but you didn't do it.'

She left them there, all innocent, in the shape that had killed him.
