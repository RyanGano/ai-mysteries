No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had four findings and one name set aside.

'Elias, Marcus, Priya, Renée,' she said. 'Four of you. The one not on my list today is Jonah.' She glanced at the technician, then back. 'Because Jonah's work, that morning, was clean. I checked it three times. His hands did the job right. Everything that killed Michael Holloway was decided above Jonah's bench, by the four of you.'

The four were silent.

'Elias—the narrowed margin, sound on paper. Marcus—the suppressed presence layer, routine on paper. Priya—the reframed hazard and the held schedule, defensible on paper. Renée—the warning filed where no one would read it in time.' She set them down. 'The scientist, the security officer, the operator, the safety lead. The four people with judgment and authority. And the one person you'll all instinctively suspect—the nervous technician—is the one who did nothing wrong.'

'Jonah was under my protection,' Priya said. 'I'd vouch for his work.'

'You don't have to,' Ellery said. 'The work vouches for itself. And that's what I want the four of you to sit with. You're the senior people. The expertise, the access, the power, the safety mandate. The four of you are what the system trusts. And the system's trust is exactly what failed. Not the junior man's hands. The senior people's judgment.'

The room was very quiet.

'There's a story this building would prefer,' Ellery said. 'The technician made a mistake. It's the easy story—blame the hands, protect the heads. I came in half-expecting to write it. And then Jonah's work turned out clean, and I had to look up, at the four of you, where the failures actually were. Calibration. Access. Schedule. Safety. The decisions of the people who decide.'

'My findings were preliminary,' Varga said.

'My flag was routine,' Rourke said.

'I made a risk call,' Priya said.

'I had escalated,' Renée said.

'Four defenses,' Ellery said. 'Each true. And together they describe four senior people each doing the defensible thing, and a man dying of it, and a junior man standing nearby with clean hands, available to blame. The report won't blame him. The report will look up.'

'It will name the four of you,' she said, 'and the four decisions, and it will note that the maintenance work itself was performed correctly.'

She stood.

'You're the ones who're supposed to know better,' she said. 'That's not an insult. It's your job. Four of you, and between you, all the knowing in the building, and it wasn't enough, and the man with the wrench is the only one who comes out clean.'

She left the four findings on the table.
