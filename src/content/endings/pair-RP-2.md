No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a disarmed alarm zone and a falsified checklist.

'Marcus,' she said, 'you carved the Charge Cage into its own alarm sub-zone, disarmed it for staging, and never re-armed it. The motion sensor that would have caught a man walking in was dark.' She turned. 'Jonah, you signed a verification sheet in nine minutes for checks that take far longer. The paper said the suit was confirmed safe. It wasn't.' She set the two records down. 'A sensor that didn't watch. A sheet that lied. Both of you, vouching for a safety that wasn't there.'

'I forgot the re-arm step,' Rourke said. 'Busy morning.'

'I signed ahead,' Jonah said, very low. 'Everybody does. There's never time.'

'Here's the shape of it,' Ellery said. 'The disarmed zone meant nothing watched the door. The false checklist meant nobody verified the suit. So Michael walks in—unwatched, because Marcus's sensor was dark—and puts on a suit certified safe by a sheet that was never really filled out. He's relying on two assurances. The sensor will catch a problem. The checklist confirms there isn't one. Both were hollow. Both were yours.'

The room was quiet.

'You two have something in common,' Ellery said. 'Neither of you lied, exactly. Marcus, you didn't decide to leave the zone dark—you forgot a step. Jonah, you didn't decide the suit was unsafe and sign anyway—you assumed it was fine, like always. You both created a false assurance through habit rather than intent. A sensor everyone thought was armed. A checklist everyone thought was real. The building ran on those two assumptions, and you'd each quietly hollowed one out.'

'A missed step isn't sabotage,' Rourke said.

'A signature isn't a death,' Jonah said.

'No,' Ellery said. 'They're both just the small ways busy people cut corners. But the building trusts the corners. Michael trusted that the zone was armed and the checklist was true, because that's what the records said. He didn't know the records were two polite fictions, written by two tired men, that happened to overlap on the morning and the suit that killed him.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the un-rearmed alarm sub-zone and the verification sheet completed in a time inconsistent with the checks, that each created a false assurance of safety, and that the victim relied on both.'

She stood.

'You both vouched for something that wasn't true,' she said. 'One with a dark sensor, one with a fast pen. Neither of you meant a thing by it. That's the part that should keep you up. It didn't take malice. It just took two corners, cut on the same morning, on the same suit.'

She left the two records on the table.
