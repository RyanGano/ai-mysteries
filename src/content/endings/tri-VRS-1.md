No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery wrote three things on the whiteboard.

'The danger. The detection. The decision to proceed,' she said. 'Three guards. One of you took down each.' She turned. 'Elias—the danger. You narrowed the discharge threshold, so the fault read as safe. Marcus—the detection. You suppressed the presence layer, so no one saw the man. Priya—the decision. You flagged the test must-run, so no one was allowed to stop.'

The three of them were silent.

'I've stopped looking for a culprit,' Ellery said. 'I'm mapping a structure now. Look at what you built between you. A suit that wouldn't flag the fault. A room that couldn't see the person. A schedule that forbade the pause. Each of you removed exactly one of the three things that had to fail. Any one of them, left standing, and Michael Holloway walks out alive.'

'These are unrelated decisions,' Varga said. 'A calibration, a security flag, a schedule—'

'Unrelated is the whole point,' Ellery said. 'You didn't coordinate. You couldn't have designed this better if you'd tried, and you didn't try. Elias makes the fault invisible. Marcus makes the man invisible. Priya makes the doubt impossible. Three people, three functions, three guards down, no conversation between any of you.'

The room was very quiet.

'Each of you will point at the other two,' Ellery said. 'Elias: the threshold only mattered because Marcus couldn't see the man and Priya wouldn't allow a halt. Marcus: the presence flag only mattered because Elias's fault didn't trip and Priya's schedule held. Priya: the must-run only mattered because the other two had already removed the safeguards. You're all three correct. Each of your contributions was harmless without the other two. That's not a defense. That's the mechanism.'

No one argued.

'The report,' she said, 'will lay out the three failures—danger, detection, decision—name the person responsible for each, and note that the death required all three and that none was coordinated with the others.'

She capped the marker and looked at the three words on the board.

'You took the suit's warning, the room's eyes, and everyone's right to say stop,' she said. 'One each. And between the three of you, there was nothing left to save him with.'

She left the three words on the board.
