No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had four findings and one empty chair.

'Four of you,' she said. 'Elias, Marcus, Priya, Jonah. And one person not on this list.' She let that sit a moment. 'Renée. Because when I traced every contributing failure, four of them ran through the four of you, and not one of them ran through her. She raised her warning, she filed her memo, she pulled the handle—a minute too late, but she pulled it. She's the only one of the five who reached for the brake.'

The four were silent. None of them looked at the empty chair.

'Elias—the narrowed threshold,' Ellery said. 'Marcus—the suppressed presence layer. Priya—the must-run schedule. Jonah—the skipped check. Four failures. The fault made invisible, the man made invisible, the doubt made impossible, the suit left unverified. Each of you took down one guard, and the four guards you took down were everything except the safety officer—and the safety officer, alone, you'd already arranged to ignore.'

'These were separate decisions,' Varga said.

'They always are,' Ellery said. 'But here's why four is different from three, or two, or one. With four of the five contributing, this stops looking like a gap that a man unluckily fell through. It starts looking like a building where almost everyone, independently, in their own lane, made the choice that served the schedule over the man. Four out of five. The exception proves it wasn't inevitable—Renée shows a different choice was available in every chair. The four of you just didn't make it.'

The room was very quiet.

'I keep returning to Renée's chair,' Ellery said. 'Because she's the proof. Each of you will say you had no choice—the schedule, the procedure, the deadline, the window. But she had the same pressures and made the other choice. Late, imperfect, overruled—but she reached for the brake. So when you tell me you couldn't have done otherwise, there's an empty chair in this room that says you could have.'

'The report,' she said, 'will document the four contributing failures and name each of you. It will note that the safety officer's actions, alone among the five, were directed at preventing the outcome.'

She stood.

'Four of you,' she said. 'Each in your own lane, each for your own reason, all toward the same morning. And one chair to show it didn't have to be that way.'

She left the four findings on the table, and the chair empty.
