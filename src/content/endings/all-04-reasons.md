No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery turned on the screen.

'Five decisions,' she said. 'Five points of leverage. I'm not going to talk about systems failing today. I'm going to talk about reasons.'

She didn't look at any one of them in particular.

'Each of you had a reason to want this morning to go a certain way,' she said. 'Not a reason to kill him. I want to be careful. A reason to look the other way. A reason to let the small wrong thing stand. And the reasons were all different, and they all pointed at the same door.'

She looked at Priya. 'You needed the milestone. He was getting cautious. He was in your way.'

Priya's mouth was a line.

She looked at Marcus. 'You had a secret he could use, and you'd been saying yes to him for years.'

Marcus didn't blink.

She looked at Elias. 'You believed the program was reckless, and you'd been right and ignored for two years, and some part of you needed to be proven right.'

Elias's jaw clenched.

She looked at Jonah. 'You were afraid. Of being fired, of being blamed, of being noticed. Fear made your hands fast and your mouth quiet.'

Jonah flinched.

She looked at Renée. 'You thought the whole program would kill thousands. You'd stopped believing your warnings worked. You'd started keeping a file instead.'

Renée didn't look away.

'Five reasons,' Ellery said. 'Succession. A secret. Vindication. Fear. A terrible arithmetic. None of them is a murder. All of them are a motive to not stop what was happening.' She paused. 'And what was happening needed exactly that. It didn't need anyone to act. It needed five people to not act, each for a private reason, at the same time.'

The room was silent.

'He was a difficult man,' Ellery said. 'I've learned that much. He kept score. He used people. Every one of you had been hurt or threatened or diminished by him at some point. And I think, when the morning came and the machine was loaded and the only thing that could stop it was one of you choosing to, every one of you found, somewhere in yourselves, a reason not to be the one.'

No one argued.

'That's the thing about a man everyone has cause to resent,' she said. 'You don't have to conspire to remove him. You just each have to fail to save him, a little, for your own reasons, on the same day. And then he's gone, and no one did it, and everyone did.'

'The report,' she said, 'will document each of your decisions on the day. It will not assign motive—motive isn't a finding, it's a shadow. But I've said the shadows out loud now, in this room, and you've heard me, and you didn't argue.'

She turned off the screen.

'You each had a reason,' she said. 'And all of you had the means. And the only thing you all agreed on, without ever agreeing, was that none of you would be the one to stop it.'

She left.

They didn't need to argue. They didn't.
