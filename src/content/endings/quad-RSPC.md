No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had four findings and one name conspicuously absent.

'Marcus, Priya, Jonah, Renée,' she said. 'The four of you. Not on my list: Elias.' She watched them take it in. 'Because for two years, Elias said this would happen. He was the one warning that the program was reckless, that the timeline was irresponsible. He was right. And four of you are the reason being right wasn't enough.'

The four were silent.

'Marcus—the suppressed presence layer. Priya—the must-run schedule. Jonah—the skipped check. Renée—the late halt.' She set them down. 'Four failures. And the one person who'd spent two years predicting exactly this is the one whose hands aren't on it. He warned. You proceeded.'

'Elias warns about everything,' Priya said. 'Being a pessimist isn't the same as being careful.'

'No,' Ellery said. 'But here's what the four of you have in common, that he doesn't. Each of you, in your own way, made the program move. Marcus, you smoothed access. Priya, you drove the schedule. Jonah, you delivered under pressure. Renée—even you, by letting your warnings be filed instead of fought, kept the machine running. The four of you are the people who, whatever your doubts, made it go. Elias is the one who kept saying stop.'

The room was very quiet.

'I want to be fair,' Ellery said. 'Elias isn't a saint. He filed his own warnings in drawers; in another version of this, he's the one I'm questioning. But on this configuration, his fingerprints aren't on the failures, and yours are. And there's something worth sitting in: the building had a person whose entire function, for two years, was to say this would happen. And the building's response was to route around him. To treat his correctness as an obstacle. The four of you each, in your lane, found a way past the man who was right.'

'I did my job,' Rourke said.

'I did mine,' Priya said.

'I did what I could,' Jonah said.

'I escalated,' Renée said.

'Four jobs, done,' Ellery said. 'And one warning, ignored, for two years, and proven right in the worst way. The report will name the four of you. It will note that the systemic risk had been repeatedly and specifically flagged—and by whom.'

She stood.

'He told you,' she said. 'For two years. The four of you found four different ways not to listen. That's the finding. Not that no one knew. That someone did, and you went around him.'

She left the four findings on the table, and beside them, two years of warnings no one had used.
