No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery put the checklist on the table.

'This is the inspection sheet for Suit Seven,' she said. 'Signed off the morning of the incident. Your signature.'

Jonah Pike looked at it. He nodded quickly.

'The grounding mesh was flagged for repair three days prior,' Ellery said. 'You completed the repair. You logged it. You ran a continuity check and it passed.'

'It passed,' Jonah said. 'I checked it twice.'

'I know.' Ellery sat down. 'I believe you.' She opened a second folder. 'This is the failure analysis. Post-incident. The grounding mesh repair was performed correctly. The continuity check confirmed the repair. The sensor pack adjacent to the mesh was also swapped during that maintenance window—routine replacement, documented.' She paused. 'The sensor pack was swapped before or after the continuity check?'

Jonah's eyes moved.

'I—after,' he said. 'I think. The way we do it—'

'The procedure calls for continuity to be re-verified after any component swap in that zone,' she said. She said it quietly. Not an accusation. A fact.

Jonah said nothing.

'There's a second signature block,' Ellery said. 'For post-swap verification. It's blank.' She turned the sheet to face him. 'You didn't have time.'

'There was—Priya had flagged the window. It was urgent.'

'I know,' she said. 'You were under pressure. You had already run one check. The repair had passed. The sensor swap was a routine part.' She folded her hands. 'You made a reasonable judgment under time pressure. The kind of judgment that gets made every day.'

He was looking at the table.

'Jonah,' she said. 'Did someone tell you to skip the second check, or did you decide?'

A long pause. 'Nobody told me to skip it. I just—it felt like it was fine.'

'It felt within tolerance.'

He flinched at the phrase. 'Yeah,' he said. 'Yeah, that.'

'The failure analysis says the sensor pack introduced a calibration offset that the system interpreted as a normal operational state,' Ellery said. 'Not a flagged state. Not a halt state. Just—normal.' She closed the folder. 'The system trusted the sensor. The sensor was off. Because the second check wasn't run.'

The room was quiet.

'I'm not here to tell you it was your fault,' she said. 'I'm here to tell you it was the missing step. And the missing step was yours to take.' She stood. 'The report will say the maintenance procedure was followed incompletely. It will name the step. It will note the time pressure.'

She picked up the checklist.

'I'm going to ask you something,' she said. 'Not for the report. For me.' She looked at him. 'Did you know the window was too short?'

His throat moved.

'Yeah,' he said. 'I knew.'

She nodded. Just once.

'That's what I needed,' she said, and she left.
