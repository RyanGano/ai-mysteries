No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had three things that vanished after the death.

'Within hours of Michael dying,' she said, 'three records were altered. Marcus—the access log, an override removed. Priya—the incident summary, curated for the board. Renée—a prior safety approval with your signature, deleted.' She set the recovery printouts down. 'Three people, three edits, one window. You all reached into the record and removed your own piece.'

The three were silent.

'I want to separate two things,' Ellery said. 'I don't know, for certain, what each of you did before the death. But I know exactly what you did after. You cleaned. All three of you, independently, within hours, identified the thing with your name on it and made it go away.'

'I tidied entries that would be misread,' Rourke said.

'I distilled a summary for the board,' Priya said.

'I removed an approval that no longer applied,' Renée said.

'Three innocent framings,' Ellery said. 'And here's what they add up to. Marcus's deletion hides how Michael got in. Priya's curation hides the pressure behind the run. Renée's deletion hides who signed off on the protocol. Together, they remove the access, the motive, and the authorization—the three things an investigator needs to reconstruct a death. Not by agreement. By three people each having the same reflex at the same time.'

The room was very quiet.

'This is what the building taught you,' Ellery said. 'When the worst happens, manage your liability. Edit the record. Curate the story. Delete the signature. It's so deeply trained that three people did it in parallel, in the same hours, without a word—and very nearly made the truth unrecoverable. I had to pull every one of these from backup. If I'd been one degree less thorough, the morning would look clean.'

'The recovered versions exist,' Priya said. 'So no harm done.'

'The harm,' Ellery said, 'is that the three of you reflexively tried to erase the access, the motive, and the authorization for a man's death, within hours of it, separately, because that's the instinct this place built into you. The cover-up wasn't planned. It was cultural. And a culture that produces three simultaneous independent cover-ups is the actual finding here.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the three post-incident alterations, their timing, the recovery of the originals, and that together they obscured access, motive, and authorization.'

She stood.

'You each protected yourselves,' she said. 'And three people protecting themselves, in the same hours, added up to a deleted morning. That's not conspiracy. That's training. It might be worse.'

She left the three recovered records on the table.
