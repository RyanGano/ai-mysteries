No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a model and a bent pin.

'Elias,' she said, 'your model filtered an anomalous reading at 09:02. It decided the sensor was lying—an outlier, noise, statistically implausible. So it smoothed it away.' She turned. 'Jonah, the reading wasn't noise. It was real. It came from a connector you'd repaired a year ago, off the books, that finally failed. The sensor saw the failure. Elias's model erased the sight.'

Jonah went pale. Varga's jaw set.

'Walk through it with me,' Ellery said. 'Jonah, a year ago you straightened a bent pin instead of flagging it. It carried current fine until the morning it didn't. When it failed, it threw exactly the kind of jagged, sudden reading a good sensor catches.' She turned to Varga. 'And your filter caught it, looked at it, and decided no real fault looks like that, it must be a glitch—and deleted it. The one true alarm of the morning, filtered out by a model that was right ninety-nine times before.'

'The reading was a statistical outlier,' Varga said. 'My filter performed correctly. It is designed to reject implausible spikes. A spike from a connector that, according to every record, was sound—'

'According to every record,' Ellery said. 'Because Jonah's repair was never recorded. So your model had no reason to expect that connector to fail. From the model's view, a fault there was impossible, so the reading had to be noise.' She paused. 'Jonah's hidden repair made the true reading look false. Your model, trusting its world, threw the true reading away. Neither of you could see the other half. He hid the cause. You filtered the symptom.'

The room was very still.

'This one's almost elegant,' Ellery said, and didn't enjoy saying it. 'Jonah, your secret created a failure no one expected. Elias, your model rejected the warning precisely because no one expected it. The unexpectedness was the whole mechanism. A logged repair, and your model would have known to watch that pin. An un-suppressed reading, and Jonah's old shortcut would have been caught at last. Instead the hidden cause and the confident filter found each other.'

'I didn't know it would fail,' Jonah said. 'I thought it was fixed.'

'And I cannot retain every reading on the chance a record is incomplete,' Varga said. 'The filter would be useless.'

'I know,' Ellery said. 'You're both telling the truth. The repair was hidden in good faith—you believed it was sound, Jonah. The reading was filtered in good faith—you believed your model, Elias. Two good-faith certainties, and between them, a man.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the unrecorded connector repair, the filtering of the resulting anomalous reading, and that the concealment of the first made the rejection of the second inevitable.'

She stood.

'You never met to do this,' she said. 'One of you hid something a year ago. One of you trusted something this morning. And a year apart, you killed a man together.'

She left the model and the pin side by side.
