No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery put a single line of code on the screen.

'One number,' she said. 'A calibration constant. It used to be 1.08. Now it's 1.8.' She turned to Elias Varga. 'A decimal point moved one place.'

'That's a rounding convention,' Varga said. 'The trailing zero was never significant to four places. Strictly speaking—'

'1.08 and 1.8 are not the same number,' Ellery said.

He stopped.

'I've read the commit history,' she said. 'You changed it in a batch of forty edits, all tagged "formatting cleanup." Thirty-nine of them were genuinely formatting. This one moved the scaling factor on the power-draw threshold by a factor of nearly two.'

'It was a transcription error,' Varga said. 'If it was anything. The kind of thing that happens in a large refactor. You're describing a typo.'

'Maybe,' Ellery said. 'A typo would be the kindest version.' She sat down across from him. 'Here's the thing about a typo, Elias. You'd have caught it. You catch everything. You correct people's grammar in meetings. You sent me an email last week to flag that I'd written "less" where I meant "fewer."'

His jaw tightened.

'So either,' she said, 'the most careful man in this building made the one mistake of his career on the one number that mattered—or he didn't make a mistake at all.'

The silence stretched.

'I don't know which I'm looking at,' Ellery said. 'And I think that's the point. You made it so I couldn't tell. A factor of two, buried in formatting, in a man who never makes errors. It's deniable in both directions. Even to you.'

Varga said nothing. His hands were flat on the table.

'The report,' she said, 'will note that the constant was altered. It will note that the alteration doubled the acceptable variance. It will note that the change was committed by you, under a label that described it as cosmetic.'

She picked up her notes.

'It will not say whether you knew,' she said. 'Because I don't think you let yourself know. That's the only way a man like you could have done it.'

She left.

The number stayed on the screen. 1.8. A point, one place to the left of where it should have been.
