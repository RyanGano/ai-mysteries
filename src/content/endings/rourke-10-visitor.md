No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had the visitor log.

'There was a guest in the building that morning,' she said. 'An investor. Came to watch the 09:30 demonstration. Signed in at 08:50.' She looked at Marcus Rourke. 'Signed in by you. And then the entry was deleted. The investor was never here, according to the record.'

'Investors come and go,' Rourke said. 'Sometimes the log doesn't get—'

'You deleted it,' Ellery said. 'Specifically. After the incident. The same way you'd clean up anything that didn't need to be seen.' She paused. 'Why does it matter whether an investor watched a demo?'

Rourke didn't answer.

'It matters,' she said, 'because of why the demo ran at all. A man came to watch a milestone. The milestone needed a result. The result needed the test. The test needed the schedule held, the window kept, the run armed on time, no matter what.' She leaned in. 'Michael wanted that investor to see something. And you knew it. And when it went wrong, the first thing you did was make the audience disappear.'

'I was protecting the company,' Rourke said. It came out before he could stop it.

There it was.

'I know,' Ellery said, gently. 'That's what you do. That's what you've always done. There's a man in the building who could spook and pull his money, and there's a death that needs to not look like what it was, and your instinct—your trained, twenty-year instinct—is to control what's seen. So you erased the witness. Not because he saw the death. Because he saw the pressure.'

The room was quiet.

'You weren't covering up a murder,' she said. 'You were covering up a motive. You were making sure no one could draw the line from the investor in the lobby to the schedule that wouldn't bend to the man who walked into the bay to make it bend.' She paused. 'But the line's there. You just made it more interesting by trying to hide it.'

Rourke looked at the log, at the blank space where the sign-in used to be.

'The report,' she said, 'will note that a visitor was present for the demonstration and that the visitor record was deleted after the incident. It will note who deleted it.'

She stood.

'You protect the company,' she said. 'I understand it now. The friendship, the favors, the cleanups—it was never about Michael, not really. It was about the place. You'd burn any single person to keep the building standing.' She paused at the door. 'Including, in the end, yourself.'

She left the visitor log on the table, with the gap in it shaped exactly like a man.
