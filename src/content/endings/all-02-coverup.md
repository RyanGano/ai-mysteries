No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had five statements.

'I took everyone's account on the first day,' she said. 'Separately. Standard. And every one of them was true.' She laid them out, a row of five. 'That's what took me so long to understand. Nobody lied to me. I kept waiting for the lie and it never came.'

The room was quiet.

'Marcus told me the door override was routine. True. Elias told me the calibration change was a permitted optimization. True. Priya told me the schedule was within her authority. True. Jonah told me the suit passed its checks. True—it did pass them. Renée told me she'd raised her concerns through proper channels. True.' She looked up. 'Five true statements. And a dead man.'

'Then there's nothing here,' Priya said. 'If every account holds—'

'Each account holds,' Ellery said. 'It's the spaces between them that don't. Marcus's true statement about the door doesn't mention that the override mattered because Renée's interlock concern was real. Renée's true statement about her concern doesn't mention that it went unanswered because Priya's schedule made it inconvenient. Priya's schedule doesn't mention that it was survivable only because Jonah cut the check. Jonah's check doesn't mention the calibration Elias had narrowed.' She paused. 'Each of you told me your piece. Each of you left out the seam where your piece met the next one. And the death lives in the seams.'

No one spoke.

'I don't think you coordinated the death,' she said. 'I think you coordinated the telling. Not in a room, not with a plan—you're too smart for that and you'd never have managed it. You coordinated it the way a building settles. Each of you, alone, instinctively, told the version of the truth that ended at the edge of your own responsibility. And five truths that each stop at the edge of their own responsibility add up to a lie that no single person ever told.'

'That's not a cover-up,' Elias said. 'Strictly speaking, a cover-up requires—'

'Coordination,' Ellery said. 'I know. That's what you're all counting on. That without coordination, there's no conspiracy, and without a conspiracy, there's just five people who each did one small defensible thing.' She gathered the statements. 'You're right. That's exactly what there is. I've never seen anything harder to prosecute. And I've never seen anything that looked more like everyone in a room deciding, without a word, to let a man take the fall for being dead.'

The room was very still.

'The report,' she said, 'will contain all five of your statements. Unedited. Every word true. And it will note, in the margins, the seams—the places where each true account stops exactly where the next person's begins.'

She stood.

'You don't have to have met to have an understanding,' she said. 'Sometimes the understanding is just that nobody looks at the seams. I looked. That's the only thing I did that you didn't.'

She left the five statements on the table, in a row, each one true.
