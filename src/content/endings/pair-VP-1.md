No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a firmware revision and a sensor pack.

'Elias,' she said, 'you remapped sensor channel seven. You resolved a documentation conflict by changing the firmware to match a drawing.' She turned. 'Jonah, you installed the physical sensor pack. You wired it to match the hardware in front of you.' She set them down together. 'He changed the map. You followed the territory. They didn't agree anymore.'

Jonah looked at the firmware revision and his face fell as he understood.

'I wired it right,' he said. 'I wired it the way it's always been wired. The pads go where the pads go.'

'You did wire it right,' Ellery said. 'By the hardware. And Elias changed the firmware so the system expected it wired the other way. Each of you was internally consistent. Elias matched the drawing. You matched the metal. And the drawing and the metal disagreed, and neither of you knew the other was working from a different truth.'

'I deferred to the canonical schematic,' Varga said. 'As one does. The firmware is downstream of the schematic.'

'And I deferred to the suit,' Jonah said, quietly. 'The actual suit. In my hands.'

'There it is,' Ellery said. 'The scientist trusts the document. The technician trusts the object. Both reasonable. Both, in their own world, correct. And the document was wrong, so Elias's change was wrong, and Jonah's hands were right, and the system believed Elias.' She paused. 'If you two had talked—if Elias had walked down to the bay and looked at the actual wiring before changing the map, or if Jonah had read the firmware notes before he buttoned up—either one of you would have caught it. The hardware says one thing, the firmware says another, somebody's wrong.'

'It's not my job to read his firmware,' Jonah said. Not defiant. Just lost.

'And it's not my function to inspect his soldering,' Varga said.

'No,' Ellery said. 'Your jobs don't overlap. That's the gap he fell through. There's a seam between the man who writes the map and the man who lays the wire, and nobody owns the seam, and the seam was wrong, and Michael Holloway leaned against a chest plate the system thought was a shoulder.'

The room was quiet.

'The report,' she said, 'will note the firmware remap and the physical installation, that each was internally consistent, that they were mutually inconsistent, and that no step verified one against the other.'

She gathered the two pieces of evidence.

'A drawing, a wire, and nobody to stand between them,' she said. 'That's the whole thing. Elias, walk down to the bay sometimes. Jonah, read the notes. The man died in the space between the page and the part.'

She left.
