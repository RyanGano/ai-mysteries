No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery stood at the end of the table with her hands in her pockets.

She had spent three weeks reading Elias Varga's notebooks. Not the ones he'd submitted — the earlier ones, the ones he kept in the bottom drawer of his desk because they embarrassed him. The ones where the math still showed its seams.

'The system worked,' she said.

No one answered.

'I want to be clear about that. The adaptive load-balancing protocol worked exactly as designed. It read the inputs it was given. It made the decision it was trained to make. When Michael Holloway walked into the Charge Cage, the system evaluated the variance in the suit's power draw, compared it against threshold, and determined the anomaly was within acceptable parameters.'

She paused.

'It was within tolerance,' she said. 'Until it wasn't.'

Elias Varga sat very still.

'The system didn't fail,' Ellery continued. 'The thresholds did. And the thresholds were yours.'

She set a single page on the table. A graph. A curve that looked almost right, until you got to the tail.

'You knew the real-world variance was wider than the model assumed,' she said. 'It's in your notebooks. Not the conclusion — you hadn't reached the conclusion yet. But the question was there. *What happens at the edge?* You wrote it down and then you moved on, because there was a deadline, and because the system passed every test you ran, and because the edge cases were rare.'

Varga's jaw moved. 'That's not—'

'You didn't build a weapon,' Ellery said. 'You built something that could become one, and you didn't finish asking the question.'

The room was very quiet.

'Michael Holloway died because the system made a decision that was correct by every measure it had been given,' she said. 'And the measures were wrong. Not maliciously. Not recklessly. Just — incompletely.'

She picked up the page.

'There's no arrest today,' she said. 'There's a report. The report says that the system performed within designed parameters. It also says the parameters were insufficient. It also says that the person most responsible for those parameters knew, at some level, that the question hadn't been fully answered.'

She looked at Varga.

'I think you've known that since the day it happened.'

He didn't deny it.

She left the page on the table when she walked out.

No one touched it for a long time.
