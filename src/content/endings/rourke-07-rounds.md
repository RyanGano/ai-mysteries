No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had Marcus Rourke's rounds log.

'You walked the floor at 09:15,' she said. 'Your route takes you past the Charge Cage. You logged it. "Nothing unusual."' She looked at him. 'Nine minutes before Michael Holloway walked into an armed bay.'

'Nothing was unusual,' Rourke said. 'At 09:15, nothing was wrong.'

'The test was armed,' Ellery said. 'The status board outside the Cage was red. Michael was in the building, on the floor, near the bay. You've worked here twenty years. You know what an armed test looks like, and you know who's supposed to be near one, and who isn't.' She paused. 'Did you see him?'

The room went quiet.

'I see a lot of people,' Rourke said. 'On rounds. I can't tell you who was where nine minutes before something I didn't know was going to happen.'

'That's a careful answer,' Ellery said. 'It's not a no.'

He didn't change it to a no.

'Here's what I think happened,' she said. 'I think you came around the corner and you saw Michael near the Cage during an armed run. And some part of you knew that was wrong. And another part of you thought—it's Michael. He knows the floor better than anyone. If he's there, there's a reason. He's not going to do anything stupid. He's been around this equipment for fifteen years.' She paused. 'And you wrote "nothing unusual," and you kept walking.'

'Even if that's true,' Rourke said, slowly, 'there's no rule that says I stop every person I see. I'd never get through a round. You assume everyone knows what they're doing. You have to. That's how a building works.'

'You have to,' Ellery agreed. 'For everyone else. Not for the man standing next to the armed bay. For that man, the whole point of you being there is that you don't assume.' She leaned forward. 'You looked right at it. The one thing you're paid to catch. And you decided it was fine, because it was him.'

Rourke looked at the log.

'I didn't think—' he started, and stopped. It was the closest he'd come to anything.

'The report,' Ellery said, 'will note that a security round passed the Charge Cage at 09:15 during an armed sequence and logged no exception. It will note that the victim was on the floor at that time.'

She stood.

'You write short sentences,' she said. '"Nothing unusual." Two words. The shortest lie in the building, and the most expensive. You saw it. You just didn't believe it could be what it was.'

She left the log open to 09:15.
