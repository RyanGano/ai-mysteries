No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a torque wrench in an evidence bag.

'This is yours,' she said. 'It was on your bench. It's out of calibration. It reads about fifteen percent low. So when it tells you a bolt is tight, the bolt is actually looser than the number says.'

Jonah Pike looked at the wrench. 'When did it—they drift,' he said. 'Torque wrenches drift. You're supposed to send them out for cal every year. This one was—' He stopped, doing the math.

'Eighteen months overdue,' Ellery said. 'There's a sticker. The date's on it.'

'I meant to send it,' Jonah said. 'You put a wrench down and you mean to send it and then you need it the next day and you use it instead, and then the next day, and it just—it never goes out. It's always the most useful tool that's most overdue, because you can't spare it.'

'The ground strap bolt,' Ellery said. 'On the suit. It has a torque spec for a reason—too loose and the connection has resistance, resistance makes heat, and the current looks for an easier path. You torqued it to spec. To the number on a wrench that reads fifteen percent low.'

'So it was loose,' Jonah said slowly. 'It was loose and the wrench told me it was tight.'

'The wrench told you it was tight,' Ellery said. 'You did everything right, according to the tool. You followed the spec. You hit the number. The number was a lie, but it wasn't your lie. It was the wrench's. And you'd known the wrench couldn't be trusted for eighteen months, and used it anyway, because it was always easier to use it than to lose it.'

The room was quiet.

'That's a small thing,' Jonah said. 'A cal sticker. Eighteen months. Everybody's got a tool that's a little overdue. You can't—nobody sends everything out on time. If you grounded every job until every tool was perfectly in cal you'd never—'

'You'd never finish,' Ellery said. 'I know. Everyone in this building has the same sentence. "You'd never finish." It's the most dangerous phrase you've got. It's true, and it's the reason for every corner anyone cut.'

Jonah looked at the wrench.

'I trusted it,' he said. 'That's the stupid thing. It's a tool. You trust your tools. You have to. You can't second-guess the wrench on every bolt.'

'No,' Ellery agreed. 'You can't. So you send it out for cal, once a year, so you can trust it the rest of the time. That's the deal. You skipped your half of the deal, and then you trusted it anyway, because trusting it was free and calibrating it cost a week without your best wrench.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note that the torque wrench used on the suit's grounding connection was significantly out of calibration and well past its service date. It will note that the connection was under-torqued as a result.'

She stood.

'You didn't fail to tighten the bolt,' she said. 'You tightened it exactly right, to a wrong number, with a tool you knew was wrong. There's a kind of innocence in that, and there isn't, and you're going to spend a long time in the space between.'

She left the wrench on the table, the cal sticker faded, eighteen months gone.
