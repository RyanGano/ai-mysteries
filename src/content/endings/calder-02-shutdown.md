No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a sequence of events on the screen.

'You initiated a safety shutdown,' she said. 'At 09:26. You saw something you didn't like and you pulled the handle. That's your job. That's exactly what you're supposed to do.' She looked at Renée Calder. 'And the shutdown is what killed him.'

Renée was very still. 'Explain that,' she said. Not defensive. Precise.

'The emergency shutdown cuts the active management of the cells,' Ellery said. 'It's designed to fail safe—drop everything to a known cold state. But the suit was mid-cycle. When you cut the management, the system dumped the stored charge the fastest way it could. Through the path of least resistance.' She paused. 'Michael Holloway was the path of least resistance.'

The room was silent.

'The shutdown assumes no one is in contact with the suit,' Ellery said. 'It's a safe state for the equipment. It was never designed for a safe state with a person inside, because a person isn't supposed to be inside during a fault. You did the textbook thing. The textbook didn't account for him.'

'I saw the anomaly,' Renée said quietly. 'The power reading was wrong. I had seconds. The procedure for an anomalous discharge is to shut down. I followed the procedure. I have followed that procedure for fifteen years.'

'I know,' Ellery said. 'You did everything right. That's the unbearable part of this one. If you'd hesitated, if you'd been the kind of person who freezes, he might have stepped back on his own. But you don't freeze. You act. You pulled the handle the instant you saw the fault, exactly as trained, and the handle routed the charge through him.'

Renée looked at the sequence on the screen. The timestamps. 09:26. The thing she'd done correctly.

'I've raised this,' she said. Her voice didn't shake; it widened. 'For years. That the emergency stop was validated without a human-in-suit case. I wrote it up. I asked for the analysis. It was deprioritized. It's in the backlog. You can find it.'

'I did find it,' Ellery said. 'You're right. You flagged the exact gap that killed him, eighteen months ago, and it was never closed. So here's where we are. You identified the hazard. You were overruled. And then you were the one who, in good faith, by the book, triggered it.' She paused. 'You were proven right in the worst possible way. By your own hand.'

The room was very quiet.

'The report,' Ellery said, 'will note that the emergency shutdown was initiated correctly and in accordance with procedure. It will note that the shutdown logic was never validated for a human-in-suit condition. It will note that this gap had been formally raised by you and not addressed.'

She stood.

'There's no fault here that's yours,' she said. 'I want to say that clearly, because you're the kind of person who will take it all anyway. You did your job perfectly. The job was the thing that was broken. You'd told them it was broken. And then it broke in your hand.'

Renée didn't answer. She was looking at the timestamp, 09:26, the moment she'd done everything right.

'You said it once,' Ellery said at the door. '"The system behaved as designed. That doesn't make it acceptable." You were talking about something else, that day. But you'll be saying it about yourself now.'

She left the sequence on the screen.
