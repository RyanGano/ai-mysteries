No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had three things people had chosen not to record.

'There's a theme,' she said. 'Elias—a fatal-case simulation, filed in drafts, never escalated. Jonah—a connector repair from a year ago, never logged. Marcus—an access override on the day, never given a reason.' She set them down. 'Three things that existed and weren't written down where they'd do any good.'

The three were silent.

'Walk it forward,' Ellery said. 'A year ago, Jonah hides a bent-pin repair. The pin slowly fails. Elias's simulation, months later, actually predicts a fault like this—but he files it in drafts. The morning comes, the pin fails, the suit faults. And Marcus's unlogged override has let the man into the bay to be there when it does.' She paused. 'A hidden cause, an unsent prediction, and an unexplained access. Three silences, stacked.'

'My repair worked for a year,' Jonah said.

'My model was preliminary,' Varga said.

'My override was routine,' Rourke said.

'Each of you had information,' Ellery said. 'Jonah, you knew that connector had a history. Elias, you'd computed that a fault like this was possible. Marcus, you knew you'd opened a door that shouldn't open mid-test. Three pieces of knowledge, in three heads, none of them shared. If any two of you had compared what you knew, the picture assembles. Jonah's hidden fault plus Elias's prediction is a flashing warning. Either plus Marcus's override is a man being let toward a known danger.'

The room was very quiet.

'You didn't lie,' Ellery said. 'You withheld. Differently, for different reasons—Jonah out of fear, Elias out of caution, Marcus out of habit. But the effect is identical: each of you held a piece of the truth privately, and the private pieces never met, and the man died in the space where they should have.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the unescalated simulation, the unrecorded repair, and the unexplained override, and that each represented withheld knowledge that, combined, predicted the incident.'

She stood.

'You each knew a part,' she said. 'A fault that was coming, a fault that was hidden, a door that was open. Nobody knew all three. He needed one of you to say your part out loud. None of you did.'

She left the three silences on the table.
