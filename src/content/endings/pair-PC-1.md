No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a falsified checklist and a closed near-miss.

'Jonah,' she said, 'you signed the pre-test verification in nine minutes—too fast to have done it. The sheet said the suit was confirmed.' She turned. 'Renée, three weeks earlier, the same fault fired in an empty bay, and you closed the near-miss without finding a root cause.' She set them down. 'One of you vouched for the suit without checking. One of you closed the case without understanding it. Two empty assurances.'

'I did the checks that mattered,' Jonah said.

'I dispositioned it per standard,' Renée said.

'Let me put them together,' Ellery said. 'Renée, your near-miss closure said, in effect, "this fault is anomalous, no recurrence trigger identified." It told the building the fault was a one-off. So when Jonah came to verify Suit Seven three weeks later, the institutional memory said there was nothing special to watch for on this unit. The very thing that should have made him check harder had been filed away as nothing.' She turned to Jonah. 'And you, trusting that the suit had no outstanding issues, signed the sheet fast, because why slow down for a suit with a clean history?'

The room was quiet.

'Your closure shaped his shortcut,' Ellery said to Renée. 'Not directly. But you set the background. "Nothing to see here." And Jonah, reading that background, saw nothing, and checked accordingly. If your near-miss had stayed open—"recurring fault, root cause unknown, verify with extra care"—he'd have read that, and maybe he doesn't sign in nine minutes. Maybe he does the post-swap check. Your fast closure and his fast signature are the same instinct, three weeks apart: this is fine, move on.'

'A near-miss can't stay open forever,' Renée said. 'And I can't be responsible for how a technician reads my dispositions.'

'A checklist gets signed under time pressure,' Jonah said. 'I can't be responsible for a near-miss I never even saw.'

'Neither of you is responsible for the other's piece,' Ellery agreed. 'That's the pattern in this whole building. But your pieces fit. Renée declares the fault a non-issue. The non-issue becomes the suit's reputation. Jonah, trusting the reputation, verifies lightly. The fault that was declared a non-issue comes back. Two people, each closing a door on the same danger—one by closing a case, one by closing a checklist—and the danger walked through the wall between the doors.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the closed near-miss with the matching fault signature and the verification sheet completed too quickly to be genuine, and that the first shaped the conditions of the second.'

She stood.

'You both decided it was nothing,' she said. 'Three weeks apart, in two different systems, about the same fault. And it was something.'

She left the two records on the table.
