No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had three documents that all predicted the morning.

'Elias—a simulation that found a fatal case, filed in drafts. Renée—a stop-work memo naming the interlock hazard, filed for the quarterly review. Marcus—' she paused. 'Marcus, yours is different. Yours is the old incident you helped bury four years ago, the near-miss that, if it had been reported, would have forced the safety review that fixed all of this.' She set the three down. 'Two warnings nobody read, and one lesson nobody learned.'

The three were quiet.

'Elias and Renée, you both saw it coming and filed it quietly,' Ellery said. 'We've been over why—caution, fatigue, the certainty of being ignored. But Marcus, yours goes back further. Four years ago there was a chance to confront this whole class of failure, and you helped make it disappear, for Michael, as a favor. If that incident had been reported, there's an audit. The audit catches the interlock gap. The gap gets fixed. None of the rest of this happens.'

'That was four years ago,' Rourke said.

'My findings were preliminary,' Varga said.

'My memo was in the record,' Renée said.

'Three time horizons,' Ellery said. 'Marcus, you removed the lesson four years out. Elias and Renée, you each declined to sound the alarm eleven days out. The deep cause and the near warnings, all suppressed by three people who each had a reason. Marcus's favor four years ago meant the system was never fixed. Elias's and Renée's silences eleven days ago meant it was never caught in time. The cover-up set the stage. The two filed warnings failed to stop the show.'

The room was very still.

'If the near-miss had been reported,' Ellery said, 'neither of your warnings would have been necessary—the gap would have been closed years ago. And if either of your warnings had been sounded, Marcus's old cover-up wouldn't have mattered—someone catches it in time. Three chances across four years. Marcus closed the first. Elias and Renée let the last two pass.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the suppressed prior incident four years ago, the unescalated simulation, and the unescalated stop-work memo, and that any one of the three, acted upon, would likely have prevented the death.'

She stood.

'Three doors,' she said. 'One closed years ago, two left shut last week. He needed any of them open. The three of you kept them all closed, for three reasons, across four years.'

She left the three documents on the table.
