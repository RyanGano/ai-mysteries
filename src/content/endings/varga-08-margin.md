No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a review comment.

'Design review, last spring,' she said. 'Someone proposed a safety margin on the discharge threshold. A buffer. A cushion between "normal" and "halt." Your comment is here.' She read it aloud. '"Excessively conservative. Recommend reducing by half. The margin reflects timidity, not data."'

Elias Varga did not flinch at his own words.

'The margin was excessively conservative,' he said. 'It was set by someone who didn't understand the physics. It cost us throughput on every run and it was justified by nothing but anxiety. Reducing it was correct.'

'It was halved,' Ellery said. 'On your recommendation. The review accepted your comment because you're the one who understands the physics. That's the whole reason you're in the room.'

'Yes.'

'The half you removed,' she said. 'That was the half Michael Holloway was standing in.'

Varga was silent.

'The discharge that killed him fell into the band you eliminated,' she said. 'Above the new threshold. Below the old one. In the cushion. The timid, anxious, excessively conservative cushion.' She set the paper down. 'It would have halted. With the original margin, it would have halted.'

'You can't know that,' Varga said. 'You're reasoning backward from an outcome. With the original margin, the system would have halted on dozens of harmless events too. False positives. Eventually someone disables a system that cries wolf, and then you have no margin at all. The conservative setting was its own hazard.'

'That's a real argument,' Ellery said. 'It might even be the right one, in general.' She paused. 'But you didn't make it in general. You made it on this threshold, and you called the people who disagreed timid, and you won, because you always win the technical argument. You're the best in the room at being right.'

'I was right,' he said. 'The margin was poorly justified.'

'The margin was poorly justified,' Ellery agreed. 'And it would have saved him. Both of those are true. I think that's the sentence you're going to be saying to yourself for the rest of your life.'

He didn't answer.

'The report,' she said, 'will note that the safety margin on the discharge threshold was reduced by fifty percent. It will note the technical rationale, which was sound. It will note that the eliminated band contained the fatal event.'

She stood.

'You weren't wrong,' she said. 'That's the worst version of this. You were right, and a man died inside the half you were right about.'

She left the comment on the table, in his own words.
