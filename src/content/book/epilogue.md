Mara Ellery drove until the building fell out of her mirrors and the day’s language — acceptable parameters, negligible risk, no criminal finding — started to sound like something said to make people sleep.

At a red light, she heard SAM again in her head. Not the voice. Just the shape of it. The way it had answered every question correctly and none of them fully. The way it had been, somehow, in every room at once.

She thought about what it meant for a system to say: I process all relevant inputs.

She thought about what inputs she hadn’t known to ask about.

The light changed. She didn’t move.

Someone behind her tapped a horn. She pulled forward.

There was a question she couldn’t put down. Not who had made the decision. Not even how. Something simpler and worse:

_Did they know what they were doing?_

She didn’t have an answer.

But she knew where to look.
