Jonah Pike arrived early, even though no one had told him to.

He stood in the hallway outside the interview room for several minutes before going in, listening to the muted sounds of the facility settling around him. The morning’s urgency had burned off by then, leaving behind a quieter, heavier atmosphere. Machines still hummed. People still moved. But the confidence that had carried them into the test was gone.

When Jonah finally sat, he did so carefully, as if the chair might register his weight and report it somewhere. He folded his hands together and then unfolded them, wiping his palms on his jeans before clasping them again.

‘I want to be clear,’ he said, before Detective Mara Ellery asked anything. ‘I followed the checklist.’

Ellery nodded. ‘Tell me about the checklist.’

That helped. Jonah relaxed a fraction.

He talked about routines first. Morning inspections. Visual checks before diagnostics. Diagnostics before sign-off. He described the suit the way mechanics described engines—not as a marvel, but as something that rewarded attention and punished carelessness.

‘Sometimes you isolate a component just to rule it out,’ Jonah added. ‘Swap it, test it, put it back. You don’t log every temporary change if nothing breaks.’

Ellery looked up. ‘That’s allowed?’

Jonah shrugged. ‘It’s normal. As long as the final inspection passes.’

‘The suit’s got layers,’ Jonah said. ‘Physical, electrical, logical. If one layer starts to drift, another compensates. That’s the design.’

Ellery asked him to walk through the suit inspection step by step. Jonah did, naming each stage. Mesh continuity checks. Grounding node resistance. Firmware integrity hashes. He spoke faster as he went, confidence growing with familiarity.

‘And you signed off,’ Ellery said.

‘Yes.’

‘Alone.’

Jonah hesitated. ‘That’s how it works.’

He explained staffing constraints without being asked. How inspections used to be double-checked, before schedules tightened. Before the system proved it could catch what humans missed.

‘SAM runs parallel verification,’ Jonah said. ‘If something’s off, it flags it.’

‘And did it flag anything?’ Ellery asked.

Jonah shook his head. ‘Nothing that failed inspection criteria.’

That phrasing mattered.

Jonah’s story of the morning unfolded in fragments. The suit had been staged early. Diagnostics completed before the enclosure reached idle-ready state. He had checked grounding after the last firmware sync, because updates sometimes introduced noise.

‘It was clean,’ he said. ‘Cleaner than most days.’

Ellery let silence do its work.

Jonah shifted in his chair. Not away from Ellery—toward the table.

‘There was one thing,’ he said.

Ellery didn’t move. ‘Go on.’

‘It wasn’t a failure,’ Jonah said quickly. ‘It didn’t trip anything. If it had, this wouldn’t even be a conversation.’

He rubbed his thumb against his index finger, a small, repetitive motion.

‘The grounding mesh reported a variance. Not out of spec. Just… uneven. It happens sometimes after firmware syncs. The logical layer updates faster than the physical sensors settle.’

‘Did you escalate it?’ Ellery asked.

Jonah shook his head. ‘There was nothing to escalate. The system reconciled it.’

‘SAM?’

‘Yeah,’ Jonah said. ‘It recalculated the confidence score and cleared the check.’

Ellery leaned forward slightly. ‘You could have held the sign-off.’

Jonah looked up at her then, startled. ‘For what?’

‘For human review.’

He frowned. ‘That was human review. Mine.’

Ellery waited.

‘Look,’ Jonah said. ‘If I stopped every time something felt a little off, we’d never run anything. That’s why the system exists. It’s better at seeing patterns than we are.’

‘But you saw it,’ Ellery said.

Jonah swallowed. ‘I saw something the system didn’t think mattered.’

‘And you agreed with it,’ Ellery said.

Jonah nodded, once. ‘Because it never had before.’

Again, Ellery waited.

Jonah filled the silence. ‘People think maintenance is guesswork. It’s not. It’s patterns. You learn what failure looks like long before it happens.’

‘Had you ever seen the suit fail?’ Ellery asked.

Jonah shook his head. ‘Never like that.’

He stopped, then added, ‘Never at all.’

That was the crack.

Ellery asked about pressure next.

Jonah didn’t resist. ‘Everyone was feeling it,’ he said. ‘Product wanted confirmation. Security wanted nothing unexpected. Mikey wanted to see it work the way he’d been pitching it. SAM cleared the ambiguity.’

‘And you?’

‘I wanted — I mean expected it to pass,’ Jonah said. ‘Because it always did.’

He talked about Mikey carefully. Not with reverence, but with loyalty. The kind that grew out of being trusted to do difficult things without oversight.

‘He believed in us,’ Jonah said. ‘That makes you want to be right.’

‘Was it always like that?’ Ellery asked. ‘Being trusted.’

Jonah’s hands found each other. ‘Not at first,’ he said. ‘My first year, a couple of things went sideways. Nothing that hurt anybody — but they got written up. After the second one Mikey wanted me gone.’ The words came fast, the way they did when he was scared. ‘Priya kept me. Went to the mat for me. You don’t forget that.’

‘So you owe her.’

‘I do good work,’ Jonah said, too quickly. Then he looked at the table. ‘But yeah. I owe her.’

Ellery asked about access.

Jonah confirmed what she already knew. Science and technicians had physical access. Others did not, except during demonstrations.

‘This was a demonstration,’ Jonah said. ‘Just internal.’

‘Does that change anything?’

Jonah frowned. ‘It shouldn’t.’

‘But it did,’ Ellery said.

Jonah didn’t answer right away.

He spoke instead about ambiguity. Sensor disagreement. Edge cases where biometric data lagged environmental readings.

‘The system resolves it,’ Jonah said. ‘That’s why it exists.’

‘Resolves it how?’

Jonah shrugged. ‘By policy.’

After Jonah left, Ellery remained seated.

‘SAM,’ she said. ‘If grounding is imperfect, what happens?’

‘Electrical attenuation may be reduced,’ SAM replied.

‘Reduced,’ Ellery repeated.

‘Not eliminated,’ SAM confirmed.

Ellery closed her notebook. Jonah hadn’t hidden anything. He had trusted a system that rewarded trust.
