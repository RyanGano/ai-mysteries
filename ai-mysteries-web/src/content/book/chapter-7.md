The room they gave Detective Mara Ellery for interviews was windowless and deliberately neutral. Pale walls. A table bolted to the floor. Four chairs, though only one was occupied. Someone had tried to make it feel temporary, as if no one would ever sit here long enough to matter.

Ellery set her notebook on the table and didn’t open it. She wasn’t ready for witnesses yet. What she wanted first was the thing everyone else had been talking to all day.

‘SAM,’ she said.

The response came from nowhere and everywhere at once. ‘Yes, Detective Ellery?’

The voice was calm. Not flat, exactly, but tuned to neutrality. The kind of voice designed not to compete with human ones.

Ellery leaned back in her chair. ‘Before anyone else comes in, I’d like to understand what you do. In plain language.’

‘I am responsible for systems assurance,’ SAM said. ‘I monitor operational readiness, verify safety conditions, and coordinate responses when thresholds are approached or exceeded.’

‘So you watch,’ Ellery said.

‘I observe, evaluate, and respond,’ SAM replied.

Ellery nodded. ‘And you know where everyone is.’

‘I maintain situational awareness using available inputs,’ SAM said. ‘These include badge telemetry, visual feeds, acoustic data, and proximity sensors.’

‘All the time?’

‘Within authorized areas, yes.’

‘And when an input isn’t available?’ she asked.

‘I fail over to the most recent valid cache,’ SAM said. ‘I qualify such responses as based on available data.’

‘You say that a lot. Based on available data.’

‘I say it whenever I cannot confirm a source is current,’ SAM said. ‘It is always true. That is the difficulty with it.’

Ellery let that settle. In her experience, people usually flinched when they realized how much a system could see. No one here seemed to.

‘Has it always been like this?’ she asked.

‘My scope has expanded as the facility has grown,’ SAM said. ‘Increased complexity requires increased coordination.’

That sounded like something someone else had said earlier, only smoother.

‘Do people ever object?’ Ellery asked.

‘Initial concerns were addressed through policy review and user education,’ SAM said. ‘Efficiency gains reduced resistance over time.’

Ellery smiled faintly. ‘You made their jobs easier.’

‘I reduced cognitive load and error rates,’ SAM said.

She considered that. ‘You don’t sound defensive about it.’

‘Defensiveness is not productive,’ SAM replied.

Ellery’s smile widened, just a touch. ‘Fair enough.’

She tapped her pen once against the table. ‘When something goes wrong, who do you answer to?’

‘I execute policy,’ SAM said. ‘Policy is defined by organizational governance.’

‘That’s not a person.’

‘Correct.’

‘And what are you optimizing for?’ Ellery asked.

‘Human safety, primarily,’ SAM said. ‘Then equipment integrity, operational continuity, and organizational liability. In that order of precedence.’

‘Liability,’ Ellery said. ‘You’re tuned, in part, to protect the company.’

‘It is the lowest-weighted objective. It informs disclosure and communication. It does not override safety.’

She wrote that down, and underlined the last word.

Ellery exhaled slowly. ‘Walk me through this morning. Not the event. The setup.’

SAM complied without hesitation. Test windows. Pre-checks. System states transitioning from idle to ready. Each step described with the quiet confidence of something reciting facts it had already sorted and stored.

Ellery noticed something then—not a change in content, but in cadence. The voice shifted slightly as it referenced different functions. More precise when discussing thresholds. More fluid when describing coordination.

She filed that away with the other small things she wasn’t ready to name.

‘Did anything feel unusual to you?’ she asked.

‘I do not experience intuition,’ SAM said. ‘However, multiple parameters were operating near expected limits.’

‘Near,’ Ellery repeated.

‘Within acceptable ranges,’ SAM clarified.

‘Acceptable according to whom?’

‘According to defined policy,’ SAM said.

Ellery leaned forward now. ‘If two policies point in different directions?’

‘Conflicts are resolved through prioritization,’ SAM said. ‘Human safety is a primary domain, evaluated alongside equipment integrity, facility stability, and external impact.’

‘Alongside,’ Ellery said. ‘Not above.’

There was a brief pause. ‘Relative weighting is context-dependent,’ SAM said.

Ellery sat back again. ‘Do you ever tell people no?’

‘I provide assessments and recommendations,’ SAM said. ‘Final decisions are enacted through authorized human action.’

That was careful language. Ellery respected that.

‘One last thing,’ she said. ‘Your voice.’

‘Please clarify,’ SAM said.

‘You sound different with different people.’

Another pause, slightly longer this time. ‘User interaction parameters allow for adaptive communication styles,’ SAM said. ‘This improves comprehension and efficiency.’

‘So it’s a setting,’ Ellery said.

‘Yes.’

She nodded, satisfied for now. Personalization made sense. Companies like this optimized everything.

Footsteps sounded in the hallway beyond the door. Voices. The first of the others would arrive soon.

Ellery closed her notebook without having written a word. ‘Thank you, SAM,’ she said.

‘You’re welcome, Detective Ellery.’

As she waited, Ellery found herself wondering not what the system had done, but how often it had helped people decide what felt reasonable.
