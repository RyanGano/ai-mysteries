No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had five folders.

She set them on the table in a row. She didn't open any of them.

'I've written five reports,' she said. 'One for each of you. I've documented what you knew, what you did, and what you chose not to do. Each report is complete. Each one describes an action that was legal, defensible, and fatal.'

No one spoke.

'Marcus,' she said. 'You toggled maintenance mode. Routine. No handoff.'

Rourke said nothing.

'Priya. You marked the window high-urgency and filed the anomaly as known risk.'

Shah's hands were still on the table.

'Elias. Your calibration thresholds were narrow. You'd written down the question you hadn't answered and moved on.'

Varga looked at the folders.

'Jonah. You skipped the post-swap verification. Time pressure. The check had already passed once.'

Pike's jaw was set.

'Renée. You wrote the stop-work memo at 08:17. You notified Priya at 08:59.'

Calder said nothing.

Ellery looked at all of them. 'None of you coordinated. None of you conspired. None of you woke up that morning intending to end a life.' She paused. 'But each of you made one decision. And the decisions touched. And the place where they touched was the Charge Cage at 09:30.'

She sat down.

'The question I kept asking myself,' she said, 'was how something this complex could happen without a plan. And then I stopped asking that question, because the answer is obvious.' She looked around the table. 'It's always like this. The system doesn't need a plan. The system has a shape, and the shape creates pressure, and pressure creates decisions, and decisions accumulate until there's an outcome that everyone contributed to and no one caused.'

The room was very quiet.

'I could file all five reports separately,' she said. 'Each one holds. Each one describes a failure with a name attached.' She looked at the row of folders. 'Or I can file the sixth report. The one that describes what happened when all five failures were present at once.'

She left the folders on the table.

'You'll want lawyers,' she said. 'All of you. Not because I'm filing criminal charges—I'm not. Because what comes next is the question of who is responsible when the answer is everyone, and that question doesn't have a procedure, and procedures are the only thing that's protected any of you up until now.'

She stood.

'Michael Holloway died because the system made a decision that was correct by every measure it had been given,' she said. 'And the measures—every single one of them—came from this room.'

She picked up her bag.

'The report,' she said at the door, 'will be thorough.'

She left the five folders on the table.

No one reached for their own.
