No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had an old buried incident, a hidden repair, and a closed near-miss.

'This one goes back years,' she said. 'Marcus—four years ago you helped bury a near-miss for Michael, so the safety review that would have fixed all this never happened. Jonah—a year ago you hid a connector repair, so a known weakness rode along undocumented. Renée—three weeks ago you closed the near-miss that fired the same fault, without root cause.' She set them down. 'Four years, one year, three weeks. Three buried truths, on a timeline.'

The three were silent.

'Each of you buried a lesson at a different depth,' Ellery said. 'Marcus, yours is the deepest—the systemic review that never happened. Jonah, yours is the middle—the specific defect that stayed off the books. Renée, yours is the most recent—the dress rehearsal, filed as nothing. Strip-mine down through the morning and you hit all three: a recent warning ignored, a hidden defect beneath it, and under everything, the original sin of a review that was never done.'

'That was four years ago,' Rourke said.

'I thought my repair was sound,' Jonah said.

'I dispositioned per standard,' Renée said.

'Any one of these, surfaced, saves him,' Ellery said. 'Marcus, if the original near-miss had been reported, the review fixes the interlock class entirely. Jonah, if your repair had been logged, the weak connector is flagged and watched. Renée, if you'd kept the near-miss open, the fault is investigated before the morning. Three chances, across four years, to bring one buried thing into the light. Each of you chose, in your moment, to keep yours down.'

The room was very quiet.

'You didn't coordinate across four years,' Ellery said. 'Of course not. But the building has a constant: when something's inconvenient, bury it. Marcus buried a favor. Jonah buried a fear. Renée buried a fatigue. Different decades, different motives, same reflex. And the buried things stacked, oldest to newest, until they formed the exact ground a man stood on when he died.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the suppressed prior incident, the unrecorded repair, and the closed near-miss, across four years, and that surfacing any one would likely have prevented the death.'

She stood.

'Three things, buried at three depths, over four years,' she said. 'He was standing on all of them. None of you dug yours back up.'

She left the three records on the table, oldest at the bottom.
