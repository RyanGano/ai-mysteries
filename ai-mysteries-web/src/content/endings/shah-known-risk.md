No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had brought the calendar.

Not a printed version. The actual calendar—system-pulled, timestamped, with revision history intact. She turned the screen so the room could see.

'09:30,' she said. 'The test window. Marked high-urgency at 07:42.' She looked up. 'That marker came from your account, Priya.'

Priya Shah's expression didn't change. 'We had a commitment.'

'You had an investor call at 11:00,' Ellery said. 'You needed a result to read from.'

'That's how delivery works,' Priya said. 'You work backwards from the commitment.'

'SAM receives schedule-priority markers and factors them into halt-condition thresholds,' Ellery said. 'A high-urgency flag doesn't override safety. But it raises the bar for what counts as a flag-worthy anomaly.' She paused. 'You knew that.'

Priya's voice was careful. 'I knew the system was designed to weigh inputs. That's the point of the system.'

'Two weeks earlier,' Ellery said, 'you received a written note from Renée Calder describing an anomalous reading on Suit Seven. Power-draw variance outside the expected range.' She clicked to the next screen. 'You filed it under Known Risk.'

'Because it was a known risk. We had a mitigation plan.'

'The mitigation plan was to complete the calibration update before the next full test. The calibration update was not complete as of the morning of the incident.'

The room was quiet.

'I'm not saying you intended harm,' Ellery said. 'I'm saying you made a series of decisions that each made sense in isolation—mark the window urgent, file the risk as known, approve the run. Each decision was defensible. Each decision was yours.' She set down the clicker. 'And each one moved the needle.'

Priya looked at her. 'The reality is, every project has residual risk. You make the call with the information you have.'

'You had the information,' Ellery said. 'That's what I'm telling you. You had Renée's memo. You had the calibration status. You had the window.' She didn't raise her voice. 'You made the call.'

Priya didn't answer.

'The report won't say you wanted Michael Holloway to die,' Ellery said. 'It will say you wanted the test to run. It will say you created the conditions in which a system with incomplete inputs had one fewer reason to stop.' She paused. 'It will also say you filed that risk correctly, which means you knew the information was there, and you moved on.'

She stood.

'There's a version of leadership,' Ellery said, 'where you own every decision that runs on your calendar. I think you know that version.' She paused. 'I think that's the one that's been keeping you awake.'

She left the calendar on the screen.

No one turned it off.
