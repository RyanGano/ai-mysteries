No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a part in an evidence bag and a disposal bin tag.

'This is a single-use contact gasket,' she said. 'It seals the high-current coupling. The spec says you fit a new one every time you open the coupling. You opened the coupling that morning.' She set the tag down. 'This gasket has been installed four times. There's a wear mark for each.'

Jonah Pike looked at it and didn't say anything for a moment.

'They're expensive,' he said finally. 'And they look fine. You pull one and it looks brand new, there's not a scratch on it, and you think, that's a perfectly good part going in the trash. So you—people keep them. There's a drawer. Everybody pulls from the drawer when stores is slow.'

'A drawer of used single-use parts,' Ellery said.

'They look fine,' Jonah said again, helplessly.

'The whole point of single-use,' Ellery said, 'is that it doesn't look fine where it matters. The wear's microscopic. The seal degrades on the inside, where you can't see it, a little more each time. By the fourth fitting it doesn't hold the way it's rated to. But it looks perfect. That's why the rule isn't "replace when worn." It's "replace every time." Because you can't tell by looking, so they took the looking out of it.'

'I know the rule,' Jonah said quietly.

'You know the rule,' Ellery said. 'And you broke it for a reason that makes a kind of sense. Throwing away a part that looks new feels like waste. Stores was slow. The budget was tight—Priya's always on about the budget. You were being thrifty. You were being a good employee, in a way. Saving the company money.'

'It seeped,' Jonah said. 'That's what happened, isn't it. The seal seeped and the current found a path it shouldn't have.'

'The seal seeped,' Ellery confirmed. 'On the fourth use. Right on schedule for a part rated for one.'

The room was very still.

'The rule was stupid, I used to think,' Jonah said. 'Throwing out good parts. I used to think whoever wrote that rule never had to answer for the budget.' He looked up. 'They wrote it for this, didn't they. For the day a part that looks fine isn't.'

'They wrote it for exactly this,' Ellery said. 'Somebody, a long time ago, learned what you just learned, and made a rule so nobody would have to learn it again. And the rule looked like waste, so you worked around it. That's how those rules die. They look like waste right up until the day they look like a man.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note that a single-use contact gasket was reused beyond its rated life. It will note the wear evidence. It will note the practice of retaining and reusing such parts.'

She stood.

'You weren't being careless,' she said. 'You were being frugal. That's the cruel part. You broke a safety rule to save money for a company that was killing itself to hit a number. You were trying to be good at the job.'

She left the gasket on the table, four wear marks deep.
