No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had an authorization form.

'The PPE interlock has a known defect,' she said. 'It nuisance-trips. Halts runs for no good reason. The fix wasn't ready, so there's a workaround: a temporary override that suppresses the trip. It requires sign-off at the director level.' She looked at Priya Shah. 'You signed it. The morning of the incident.'

'The interlock was halting valid runs,' Priya said. 'It was costing us days. The override was an approved mitigation while engineering finished the real fix. Signing it was within my authority and consistent with the documented process. There's a form precisely because it's a permitted action.'

'The interlock,' Ellery said, 'is the thing that stops the suit from discharging when a person is in contact with it. The override suppresses the trip. You signed away the one protection that would have stopped what happened.'

'The override suppresses false trips,' Priya said. 'It was never meant to suppress a real one. Those are different events. The whole premise of the workaround is that the trips it was firing were false.'

'How do you suppress only the false ones,' Ellery said, 'in advance, before you know which is which?'

Priya didn't answer.

'You can't,' Ellery said. 'That's the thing. The override doesn't know the difference. It can't. It suppresses the trip. All of them. The harmless ones you were trying to silence, and the one that wasn't harmless at all. When you signed that form, you signed for both, because there was never a way to sign for only one.'

The room was quiet.

'I had engineering's recommendation,' Priya said. 'Elias's team flagged the nuisance trips as the higher operational risk. I acted on the technical advice I was given. That's what a director does.'

'You acted on the advice that let the schedule hold,' Ellery said. 'There were two recommendations on your desk that week. Engineering's, that the trips were a nuisance. And Renée's, that the interlock shouldn't be touched until the fix was in. You signed the one that kept you moving.'

Priya's jaw tightened.

'That's not a failure,' she said. 'That's a judgment call between two competing risks. That's literally the job.'

'It is the job,' Ellery agreed. 'And you did it. You weighed throughput against protection, and you put your name on protection coming off.' She set the form down. 'The man it was protecting walked in two hours later.'

She gathered her things.

'The report will note that the PPE interlock trip was suppressed under a temporary override, that the override was authorized at director level, and that it was active at the moment of the incident.'

She stood.

'You called it risk,' she said. 'That's your word for it. "That's not a failure, that's risk." You signed the form that turned the risk into a man.'

She left the authorization on the table, your signature at the bottom.
