No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had an email thread and a halt record.

'Renée,' she said, 'ten days before the incident you raised the interlock as a hazard and asked for the program to pause.' She turned. 'Priya, you replied, reframed it as "risk appetite," and recommended proceeding.' She set them down. 'And then, the morning of the incident, the run went ahead, and you, Renée, didn't invoke a halt.'

'I made a documented risk decision,' Priya said.

'I had raised it through the proper channel,' Renée said. 'It was overruled.'

'Here's the mechanism,' Ellery said. 'Renée, you warn. Priya, you reframe the warning into a managed risk. And the reframing does something specific to you, Renée—it tells you the warning has been heard and decided. The system has spoken. Your concern is now a logged risk that leadership has accepted. And once it's a logged, accepted risk, what's the point of pulling a handle? The decision's been made above you.' She paused. 'Priya's reframe didn't just dismiss your concern. It converted it into a reason for you to stand down.'

The room was quiet.

'You two have done this dance for years,' Ellery said. 'Renée raises a hazard. Priya turns it into an accepted risk. Renée, having been formally overruled, stops fighting—because fighting an accepted risk just marks her as the obstacle. It's worked, in the sense that the program kept moving and no one got hurt. Until the morning the accepted risk was real.'

'I can't halt every run I've ever flagged,' Renée said. 'Once a concern is adjudicated, continuing to resist it isn't safety, it's insubordination. I escalated. It was decided against me. That's the process working.'

'That's the process working,' Ellery agreed. 'And the process working is what killed him. Priya, your reframe was the instrument that turned a stop sign into a speed bump. Renée, your respect for the adjudicated decision was the instrument that kept you in your chair when the handle was right there. Neither of you did anything outside the process. The process itself was the weapon. You aimed it together—one by reframing, one by deferring.'

'So the answer is I should have defied a decision that had been made?' Renée said.

'The answer,' Ellery said, 'is that when Priya reframed your hazard as a risk appetite, you let her relabel you out of the fight, and you've let her do it for years, and this time the fight was the one that mattered. Priya knows your warnings can be neutralized with the right word. You've taught her that, by always accepting the word. Between you, you've built a reliable machine for turning safety concerns into accepted risks. It ran perfectly that morning.'

'The report,' she said, 'will include the reframed warning and the un-invoked halt, and note that the reframing of the hazard contributed to the decision not to halt.'

She stood.

'One of you turns warnings into risks,' she said. 'One of you accepts the relabeling. Together you make sure no warning ever quite becomes a stop. He died inside the gap between a hazard and an appetite.'

She left the thread and the halt record on the table.
