No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had two graphs.

'This is what your model predicted,' she said, pointing to the first. 'And this is what the sensors actually reported.' She pointed to the second. 'On the morning of the incident, they disagreed. The sensors said something was wrong. Your model said the sensors were wrong.'

'The sensors are noisy,' Elias Varga said. 'That is not a controversial statement. They produce spurious readings constantly. The model exists precisely to filter that noise. A reading that contradicts a well-validated model is, far more often than not, an artifact.'

'Far more often than not,' Ellery repeated. 'How often is not?'

He didn't answer.

'There's a flag in the log,' she said. 'An anomalous power reading from Suit Seven at 09:02. The raw sensor caught it. And then the filter—your filter—smoothed it away. Reclassified it as noise. Because the model said a reading like that, at that moment, couldn't be real.'

'The model was correct ninety-nine times out of a hundred,' Varga said. 'You're holding up the hundredth and calling it a failure of judgment. It was a failure of luck. The model performed exactly as designed. It rejected an outlier. That is what filters do.'

'It rejected the one outlier that was a man dying,' Ellery said.

The room was very quiet.

'You built something better than the sensors,' she said. 'That's not a small thing. Your model really is more reliable than the raw data, most of the time, and you have the validation to prove it. So you taught the system to trust you over its own eyes.' She paused. 'And then its eyes were right, and you weren't, and the system did exactly what you'd taught it: it believed you.'

'You're describing the correct behavior,' Varga said. His voice had gone quiet. 'A system that chases every raw anomaly is useless. You have to trust the model. There's no other way to build it.'

'I believe you,' Ellery said. 'I think that's the tragedy. There may not have been another way to build it. You did it the only way it could be done, and the only way it could be done had this in it the whole time.'

She gathered the graphs.

'The report will note that the anomalous reading was filtered as noise by a validated model. It will note that the reading was not noise.' She paused. 'It won't assign blame, exactly. There isn't a rule you broke. There's just a man who trusted his own work more than the world, the way he always had, on the one day the world was telling the truth.'

She left.
