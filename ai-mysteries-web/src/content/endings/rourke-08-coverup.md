No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had two versions of the same log.

'This is the access record I was given on day one,' she said, laying down the first. 'And this is the one I pulled from the backup server myself, this week.' She laid down the second. 'They don't match.'

Marcus Rourke looked at them and said nothing.

'In the first one,' she said, 'the Charge Cage door shows no activity in the incident window. Clean. In the second one—the one you didn't know I'd find—there are three entries you'd removed. A door cycle. An override. A manual log edit at 11:30, ninety minutes after Michael Holloway died.'

'Logs get cleaned,' Rourke said. 'Maintenance. Old entries—'

'You edited the record of the morning a man died,' Ellery said. 'Ninety minutes after. While he was still—' she stopped. 'You sat down at the console and you took things out.'

The room was very quiet.

'I want to be fair to you,' she said. 'Editing the log doesn't mean you killed him. I've thought about that a great deal. A man can be innocent of the death and guilty of the cover-up. They're different crimes. Sometimes the cover-up is just fear.' She looked at him. 'Was it fear?'

Rourke's jaw worked.

'You panicked,' she said. 'That's my read. Michael's dead, and you're the security officer, and you know the door was overridden and the alert was muted and the footage has a gap, and all of it routes through your console. And you knew how it would look. So you cleaned it. Not to hide a murder. To hide that it could be read as one.'

'I didn't do anything to him,' Rourke said. The first flat denial of the morning.

'I believe you might be telling the truth about that,' Ellery said. 'But here's the problem with cleaning the log. The original's gone. So I can't see what really happened in that window anymore. You erased the only record that could have cleared you. To protect yourself, you destroyed your own alibi.' She paused. 'Now there's just the edit. And the edit is the most incriminating thing in the building.'

He looked at the two logs, the clean lie and the messy truth.

'The report,' she said, 'will note that the access record was altered after the incident. It will note the timestamp of the alteration. It will note that the original entries, recovered from backup, included an interlock override.'

She stood.

'You spent twenty years keeping this place's secrets,' she said. 'And when it counted, you couldn't stop. You cleaned up the one mess that needed to stay exactly as it was. That's the habit that's going to undo you. Not what you did that morning. What you couldn't stop yourself from doing after.'

She left both versions on the table, side by side.
