No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a maintenance schedule with a red mark on it.

'The suit needed a service window before the test,' she said. 'Sensor swap, grounding check, the usual. The procedure budgets ninety minutes. You compressed it to forty.' She looked at Priya Shah. 'You moved the test up.'

'I moved the test to align with a stakeholder's availability,' Priya said. 'The maintenance window adjusted accordingly. The technician confirmed he could complete the work in the time available. I don't do the maintenance. I set the schedule and the team tells me if it's feasible.'

'Did Jonah Pike tell you it was feasible,' Ellery said, 'or did he tell you he'd try?'

The room went quiet.

'Because there's a difference,' Ellery said. 'Feasible means the work fits. "I'll try" means a man is going to cut corners to make your number, and you both know it, and neither of you says it out loud. Forty minutes for a ninety-minute job isn't a schedule. It's a dare.'

'I gave him a constraint,' Priya said. 'Constraints are how things get done. If I budgeted ninety minutes for every ninety-minute job, we'd ship in a decade. You push. People find efficiencies. That's not recklessness, that's operational discipline.'

'The efficiency he found,' Ellery said, 'was skipping the second verification on the sensor pack. Because there wasn't time. Because you'd taken the time.' She paused. 'He didn't tell you he was going to skip it. He didn't have to. You handed him forty minutes and a job that takes ninety, and you let the arithmetic make the decision so neither of you had to.'

Priya was silent.

'You didn't tell him to skip the check,' Ellery said. 'I want to be precise. You never said those words. You just removed the fifty minutes the check lived in. The instruction was in the calendar. He read it correctly.'

'A schedule isn't an order to be unsafe,' Priya said. 'If he couldn't do it safely in the time, his job was to say so and refuse. That's on him. I can't be responsible for every shortcut a technician takes under pressure.'

'You can't be responsible for the shortcut,' Ellery agreed. 'You're responsible for the pressure. He's a man who was terrified of losing this job. You knew that—it's why you protected him, it's why he's loyal to you. And you handed that man an impossible window and counted on him being too scared to say no.' She set the schedule down. 'You weaponized his loyalty against his judgment.'

The room was very still.

'The report,' she said, 'will note that the pre-test maintenance window was reduced from ninety minutes to forty by schedule revision. It will note who revised it. It will note the verification step that was omitted in the time remaining.'

She stood.

'You're going to say you didn't touch the suit,' she said. 'And you didn't. You touched the clock. It turns out that's enough.'

She left the schedule on the table, forty minutes circled in red.
