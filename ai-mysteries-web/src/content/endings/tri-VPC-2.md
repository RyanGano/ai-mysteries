No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had three things that were almost right.

'Elias—your sensor remap, made to match a drawing that turned out wrong. Jonah—your checklist, signed in nine minutes for checks that take longer. Renée—your near-miss closure, three weeks early, "no root cause identified."' She set them down. 'A wrong correction, a hollow verification, and a closed warning. Each one looks like diligence. None of it was.'

The three were silent.

'This is the one where everyone did their job on paper,' Ellery said. 'Elias, you resolved a documentation conflict—looks like care. Jonah, you completed the verification sheet—looks like care. Renée, you dispositioned the near-miss—looks like care. Three acts of apparent diligence, three boxes ticked, and inside each box, nothing real. A correction toward a wrong drawing. A checklist signed without checking. A case closed without understanding.'

'I deferred to the canonical schematic,' Varga said.

'I did the checks that mattered,' Jonah said.

'I dispositioned per standard,' Renée said.

'Here's how they reinforce each other,' Ellery said. 'Renée, your near-miss closure told the building Suit Seven had no outstanding issues. So Jonah, verifying it, saw a clean-history suit and signed fast. And Elias's remap had introduced an error that a real verification might have caught—if Jonah had actually performed one, instead of signing a sheet about a suit Renée had declared fine.' She paused. 'Your closure licensed his shortcut. His shortcut missed Elias's error. Three forms of looking-without-seeing, each making the next one feel safe.'

The room was very quiet.

'The records are immaculate,' Ellery said. 'That's what's chilling. If I'd trusted the paperwork, I'd have found a properly resolved documentation conflict, a completed verification sheet, and a closed near-miss. A model of process. Three people doing exactly what the system asks. And the system asks for the form to be filled, not for the thing to be true. You all filled the form. None of you did the thing.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the firmware remap toward an erroneous drawing, the verification sheet completed too quickly to be genuine, and the near-miss closed without root cause, and that each was procedurally complete and substantively empty.'

She stood.

'You all followed the process,' she said. 'Perfectly. The process was three boxes, and you ticked all three, and behind every box was a thing nobody actually did.'

She left the three immaculate records on the table.
