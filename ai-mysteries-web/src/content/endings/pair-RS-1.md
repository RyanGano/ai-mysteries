No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had an access grant and a must-run flag.

'Marcus,' she said, 'you gave Michael Holloway deep-tier access to the Charge Cage. His badge opened a door it shouldn't have.' She turned. 'Priya, you flagged the test must-run and put an investor in the building to watch it. Michael wanted to be in that room when the milestone hit—for the investor, for the moment.' She set the two records down. 'You gave him the key. You gave him the reason.'

'I granted access at his request,' Rourke said. 'He was leadership.'

'I scheduled a demonstration,' Priya said. 'That's my function.'

'Between you,' Ellery said, 'you put a man with no operational role inside an armed test bay at the exact moment of maximum danger. Marcus, your access let him through the door. Priya, your milestone gave him somewhere he wanted to be. Neither alone does it. A man with access and no reason to be there stays at his desk. A man with a reason and no access stands behind the glass. He needed both. You provided one each.'

The room was quiet.

'Did you two talk about this?' Ellery asked. 'About Michael being on the floor for the demo?'

A pause. Then Priya: 'He mentioned wanting to be present. I may have said the access should be sorted out.'

'I sorted it out,' Rourke said.

'There it is,' Ellery said. 'Not a conspiracy. A logistics problem, solved. Michael wants to be in the room for the investor's benefit. Priya wants the demo to land. Marcus makes the access happen. Three people coordinating something completely ordinary—getting the founder into the room for the big moment. And the big moment was the most dangerous instant of the week, and the room was the one that kills you, and you'd smoothed his path right into it.'

'We were getting him to a demonstration,' Priya said. 'Not to his death. No one could have known—'

'You're right,' Ellery said. 'No one knew. That's not the charge. The charge is that you two, between you, removed every obstacle between Michael Holloway and the inside of that bay. The door, and the reason. You made it frictionless for him to be in the worst possible place. You were being accommodating. You were being good at your jobs. And accommodation, that morning, was a path that led exactly one place.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the access grant and the must-run demonstration, that together they placed the victim inside the armed bay, and that each was arranged independently in service of the same event.'

She stood.

'You cleared his way,' she said. 'Both of you. One opened the door, one lit the reason. He walked the path you'd smoothed, straight in.'

She left the access grant and the flag side by side.
