No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a safety margin and a side agreement.

'Elias,' she said, 'you halved the discharge margin. You called the original setting timid. The reduction was technically sound and it cost the system its cushion.' She turned. 'Priya, you'd signed a delivery commitment the science couldn't meet on time. You needed throughput. A wider margin meant slower runs. A narrower one meant hitting your date.'

'I made an engineering judgment,' Varga said. 'On the merits. The margin was poorly justified.'

'You did,' Ellery said. 'On the merits. And the merits happened to align, perfectly, with what Priya needed.' She looked at Shah. 'You didn't tell him to cut the margin. You didn't have to. You'd created an environment where the throughput-positive answer was always going to win the technical argument, and Elias is a man who loves winning technical arguments. You set the incentives. He provided the rationale. Neither of you had to say the quiet part.'

'That's insulting to us both,' Priya said. 'You're suggesting he compromised his science to serve my schedule, and that I manipulated him into it. Elias would never bend his analysis, and I would never ask him to.'

'I'm suggesting something worse,' Ellery said. 'That nobody bent anything. That Elias gave his honest technical opinion, and his honest technical opinion happened to be the one that served your deadline, and so it sailed through with your full support, when a less convenient opinion would have met resistance. The system selected for the answer that helped the schedule. It didn't have to corrupt anyone. It just had to reward one conclusion over the other.'

The room was quiet.

'Here's the test,' Ellery said. 'Elias, if the timid margin had been the throughput-positive one, and the aggressive cut had been the slow one—would you have fought as hard to cut it? Be honest. You don't have to answer out loud.'

Varga didn't answer out loud. Something moved behind his eyes.

'And Priya,' Ellery said, 'if Elias had recommended widening the margin, slowing every run, missing your date—would his recommendation have moved as smoothly through review?'

Priya's jaw was tight.

'You didn't conspire,' Ellery said. 'You did something the law has no name for. A scientist who's sure he's objective, and an operator who shapes which objectivity gets rewarded. Between you, you produced a margin cut that was simultaneously good science and good for the schedule and fatal for the man who stood in the half you removed.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the margin reduction and its technical rationale, the undisclosed delivery commitment, and the alignment between them. It will name you both.'

She stood.

'The most dangerous decisions in this building,' she said, 'aren't the corrupt ones. They're the ones where being right and being convenient turn out to be the same answer, and nobody can tell which one they were actually serving.'

She left.
