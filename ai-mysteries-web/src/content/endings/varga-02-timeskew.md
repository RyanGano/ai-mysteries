No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had two clocks on the screen.

'These should read the same time,' she said. 'The control system and the suit telemetry. They're supposed to be synced to the same source.' She pointed. 'They're four hundred milliseconds apart.'

Elias Varga looked at the numbers the way other people looked at a misplaced apostrophe.

'That's a configuration detail,' he said. 'Strictly speaking, the synchronization tolerance permits—'

'I know what it permits,' Ellery said. 'I'm asking what you changed.'

Varga was quiet for a moment. 'I adjusted the sync interval. In March. The previous setting introduced jitter into the data pipeline that was contaminating my variance analysis.' He said it precisely, as if precision were the same thing as innocence. 'It was a routine optimization.'

'Four hundred milliseconds,' Ellery said. 'How long does it take a safety interlock to decide a person isn't where the sensor says they are?'

He didn't answer.

'The system saw Michael Holloway,' she said. 'And then, four hundred milliseconds later, it saw a power state that didn't include him. And because the clocks disagreed, it couldn't tell those were the same moment. So it averaged them.' She paused. 'It split the difference between a man being there and a man not being there.'

'You're conflating two systems,' Varga said. His voice had thinned. 'The interlock logic and the telemetry sync are—'

'They're the same clock now,' Ellery said. 'You made them the same clock. That was the optimization.'

The room was very quiet.

'It was within tolerance,' Varga said, almost to himself. 'The specification allowed it.'

'Yes,' Ellery said. 'The specification did.' She closed the laptop. 'The report will say the synchronization fell within designed parameters. It will also say the parameters assumed no human in the loop at the moment of divergence. It will also say you knew the pipeline carried more than data.'

She stood.

'You corrected the jitter,' she said. 'You always correct things. I think that's the part you can't forgive yourself for. Not that you were careless. That you were careful, and it didn't matter.'

She left the two clocks running on the screen.

They never quite agreed.
