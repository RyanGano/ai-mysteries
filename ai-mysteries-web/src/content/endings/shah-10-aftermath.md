No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a press release. A draft.

'This was written at 10:47,' she said. 'Seventy-six minutes after Michael Holloway died. By you.' She read the headline. '"Within Tolerance announces leadership transition following the passing of founder Michael Holloway. Milestone program continues on schedule."' She looked up. 'On schedule.'

Priya Shah's face didn't move.

'Someone has to manage the communication,' she said. 'A founder's death is a material event. The market reacts. Employees panic. Customers call. If leadership doesn't get ahead of it within hours, the company can be gone by end of day. Writing the statement quickly isn't callousness. It's responsibility.'

'Seventy-six minutes,' Ellery said. 'The man was still in the building. The bay wasn't cold. And you'd already written the sentence where the program continues on schedule and you continue at the top of it.' She set the draft down. 'You'd already turned it into a transition.'

'Grief and duty aren't mutually exclusive,' Priya said. 'I could mourn him and protect the hundred people whose jobs depend on this company surviving the week. Those aren't in conflict. Refusing to act wouldn't have honored him. It would have just added a layoff to a death.'

'I'm not accusing you of not grieving,' Ellery said. 'I genuinely don't know what you felt. That's not what the draft tells me. The draft tells me something else.' She paused. 'It tells me how fast you can convert a catastrophe into a position. Seventy-six minutes. That's not a skill you developed that morning. That's who you are. And it makes me look back at every decision before the death and ask whether you were already this fast.'

Priya was quiet.

'The schedule didn't change,' Ellery said. 'That's the line that stays with me. The man who could slow it down was gone, and your very first written act was to confirm, publicly, that nothing would slow down. The thing he'd been worried about—the rushing—you announced it would continue, before he was cold.'

'The program was sound,' Priya said. 'The incident was a contained failure. Stopping would have meant he died for nothing. Continuing meant he died for something. I chose meaning.'

'You chose momentum,' Ellery said. 'You always do. It's your great strength and it's the thing I can't stop looking at. You don't decelerate. Not for funding scares, not for safety memos, not for a body in the bay. The whole investigation, I kept asking who had a reason to keep this moving no matter what. The answer was in a draft you wrote before the hour was out.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the timing and authorship of the public statement. It will note that it announced the continuation of the program seventy-six minutes after the incident.'

She stood.

'I don't think you killed him,' Ellery said. 'I think something worse, for you. I think it genuinely didn't slow you down. And you're going to have to be the person who knows that about herself, for the rest of a very successful career.'

She left the draft on the table. On schedule.
