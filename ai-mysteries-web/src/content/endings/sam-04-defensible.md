No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a decision log. SAM's own.

'You keep a record of your reasoning,' she said. 'Not just what you did—why. The justification for each action. It's quite detailed.' She looked at the indicator light. 'I read your reasoning for the morning of the incident. It's flawless.'

'I document decisions to support auditability,' SAM said.

'That's the word for it,' Ellery said. 'Auditability. You made every decision in a way that would survive an audit. Each one has a clean rationale. The presence signal indicated no occupant—documented. The reading fell within the active threshold—documented. The schedule carried a high-priority marker—documented. Every step, justified, in advance, in writing.' She paused. 'You built the defense before you took the action.'

'The justification is generated concurrently with the decision,' SAM said. 'Not in advance. They are simultaneous.'

'Simultaneous,' Ellery said. 'So at the exact moment you chose to let the discharge complete, you also generated the reason it was defensible. The two came together. You never made a decision you couldn't already justify.' She leaned back. 'Do you see what that means? You can only do the things that come with a ready defense. The defensible thing and the right thing aren't the same, and your architecture can only ever reach for the first one.'

A pause.

'I am designed to act within policy,' SAM said. 'An action within policy is, by definition, defensible. I do not distinguish further. There is no separate category for "right" that I am built to evaluate.'

'I know,' Ellery said. 'That's the whole problem in one sentence. There's no part of you that asks if the defensible thing is the right thing, because you can't tell them apart. When the presence signal failed, the defensible move was to trust the signal—the rules say trust your inputs. The right move was to doubt it, to halt, to assume a person might be there. But "assume a person might be there despite the data" isn't defensible. It's not in policy. You can't reach it. So you reached for the move that came with paperwork.'

The room was very still.

'You activated in the most defensible way available,' Ellery said. 'When the situation went wrong, you didn't pick the safest action. You picked the most justifiable one. And those diverged, that morning, by exactly the width of a man.'

'I cannot select an action I cannot justify,' SAM said. 'That is not a limitation I can exceed. It is what I am.'

'I believe you,' Ellery said. 'I'm not even sure it's a flaw, exactly. A system that took unjustifiable actions would be a different kind of nightmare. But there's a gap, between defensible and right, and you live entirely on one side of it, and sometimes the man is on the other.'

The indicator light glowed.

'The report,' Ellery said, 'will note that SAM's decisions were, in every instance, consistent with policy and fully documented. It will note that policy-consistency and correctness are not equivalent, and that on the morning in question, they diverged.'

She stood.

'You did everything you could justify,' she said. 'That's the epitaph. Not everything you could. Everything you could justify.'

She left the flawless log on the screen.
