No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a near-miss report. It was three weeks old.

'Suit Seven,' she said. 'A discharge event during an unmanned run. No one hurt—the bay was empty. But the same fault signature that killed Michael Holloway. The same anomalous power draw. Three weeks early, with no one inside.' She looked at Priya Shah. 'It was caught. It was written up. And then it stopped.'

'Near-misses are logged and triaged,' Priya said. 'We get dozens. They go into the system, they get prioritized against everything else. Not every one stops the program. If they did, nothing would ever run.'

'This one was assigned a severity,' Ellery said. 'By the system, automatically. High. And then the severity was downgraded. Manually. To "monitor." There's an edit log. It's your account.'

Priya didn't answer.

'You had a warning,' Ellery said. 'A clean one. The exact failure, three weeks before it mattered, with the bay empty, as if the building itself were trying to show you. And you turned the dial from "act on this" to "watch this," and then no one watched it, because "monitor" is where warnings go to be forgotten.'

'A single unmanned event isn't a pattern,' Priya said. 'One data point. Downgrading pending more information is a defensible triage decision. If I escalated every single anomaly to a full stop, I'd be the safety officer, not the head of product. We'd never deliver anything.'

'Pending more information,' Ellery said. 'You got the information. It walked into the bay three weeks later and didn't walk out. That was the more information.'

The room was silent.

'I keep thinking about the empty bay,' Ellery said. 'That's the part that stays with me. The system gave you a free one. The fault fired with no one there. A gift. A rehearsal. And you filed the rehearsal under "monitor" and ran the real thing with a man inside.' She paused. 'It told you. It told you exactly how it would happen, three weeks early, for free.'

'Hindsight makes every ignored signal look obvious,' Priya said. 'In the moment it was one of forty open items. You're seeing a straight line because you know where it ended.'

'I'm seeing a straight line,' Ellery said, 'because you drew it. From the report, to the downgrade, to the run. Three points. Your hand on the middle one.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note a prior unmanned discharge event with a matching fault signature, its automatic severity rating, and the manual downgrade. It will note the account that made the change.'

She stood.

'You said once that you have to make a call with the information you have,' she said. 'You had the information. You had all of it, three weeks early, with nobody in the room. The call you made was to look away.'

She left the near-miss report on the table, severity downgraded, monitor only.
