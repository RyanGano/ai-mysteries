No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a list of suppressed alerts.

'The proximity system throws warnings,' she said. 'Someone gets close to an armed bay, it pings Security. Most of them are nothing. People walking past. The system cries wolf a hundred times a day.' She looked at Marcus Rourke. 'You have a filter. A nuisance filter. It auto-dismisses the ones you've decided don't matter.'

'You have to filter them,' Rourke said. 'Or the panel's useless. It's all noise. You'd never see a real one.'

'That's a reasonable thing to do,' Ellery said. 'Tune out the false alarms so you can hear the true one.' She paused. 'The Charge Cage proximity alert is on your filter list. You suppressed it. Permanently. Three weeks ago.'

Rourke said nothing.

'Why that one?'

'It went off constantly,' he said. 'The Cage is next to the corridor. People pass it all day. Every one of them set it off. It was the worst offender on the board. I muted it because it was meaningless.'

'It was meaningless,' Ellery said, 'until it meant a man was standing in an armed bay and no one got the ping.' She set the list down. 'The one alert that would have told someone Michael Holloway was where he shouldn't be—you'd already decided it never told the truth.'

'It never did,' Rourke said. 'Not once in three weeks. You're describing a system that lied a thousand times and told the truth once, and asking why I stopped listening.'

'I know,' she said. 'That's exactly what I'm asking. Because that's the trap, isn't it. The alert that goes off for nothing a thousand times is the same alert that goes off for everything the one time. And you can't tell them apart in advance. You can only tell afterward. By the body.'

The room was quiet.

'You made a defensible call,' she said. 'Mute the boy who cried wolf. Anyone running that board would have done it. The manufacturer probably recommends it.' She paused. 'And the wolf came.'

Rourke looked at the list, at the line item with his name and the timestamp.

'The report,' Ellery said, 'will note that the Charge Cage proximity alert was suppressed at the security console three weeks prior to the incident. It will note that the suppression was within standard nuisance-management practice. It will note that no proximity warning was generated on the morning of the incident, because there was no longer any way for one to be.'

She stood.

'You did your job,' she said. 'You cleaned up the noise so you could hear the signal. And then the signal came through the channel you'd already cleaned away.'

She left the list behind.
