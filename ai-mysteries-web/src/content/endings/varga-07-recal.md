No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a maintenance schedule.

'The interlock recalibration,' she said. 'It runs every ninety days. The last one was due the week of the incident. You owned it.' She looked at Elias Varga. 'You moved it.'

'I rescheduled it,' Varga said. 'The recalibration requires the rig to be offline for six hours. The test window made that impossible. I pushed it to the following week. That is a scheduling decision, not a—'

'The recalibration corrects for sensor drift,' Ellery said. 'Over ninety days, the readings wander. The procedure pulls them back to true. You know this better than anyone—you wrote the procedure.'

'I did.'

'So on the morning of the incident, the suit was running on calibration that was ninety-one days old. One day past due. Drifted.' She paused. 'And you knew exactly how far it would have drifted, because you've measured it every quarter for years.'

Varga said nothing.

'How far, Elias?'

'It varies,' he said. 'The drift is not linear. Strictly speaking, on a given unit, at ninety-one days, the expected deviation is—' he stopped.

'Enough,' Ellery said. 'It was enough.'

The room was very quiet.

'You had a choice,' she said. 'Run the recalibration and lose the test window, or run the test on drifted sensors. You chose the test window. You—you, of all of them, the one who's spent two years saying they rush.' She watched him. 'You rushed.'

'I deferred a maintenance task by one week,' Varga said. 'That is the most defensible decision in this entire investigation. It's in the procedure manual as a permitted deferral. The manual that I wrote, yes—and the manual permits it.'

'The manual permits a one-week deferral,' Ellery said. 'It assumes the intervening test is non-critical. It assumes no one will be inside the suit at the edge of the drifted range.' She leaned forward. 'You wrote the permission, and you wrote the assumption, and on the morning it mattered you remembered the permission and forgot the assumption.'

He looked at the schedule.

'It was within tolerance,' he said. 'By a day. One day.'

'I know,' she said. 'That's what makes it yours.'

She left the schedule on the table, the recalibration date circled, one day late.
