No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a photograph of the inside of the suit.

'The grounding mesh,' she said. 'It carries stray current away from the body. It's the layer that means a fault goes to ground instead of through the person.' She pointed at the photo. 'This section was repaired. The repair used the wrong mesh.'

Jonah Pike leaned in, then sat back. 'That's—the gauge looks close,' he said. 'It looks right. They're almost the same.'

'Almost,' Ellery said. 'One's the spec material. One's a substitute from the general stores. They look identical. They don't conduct identically.'

'We were out,' Jonah said. The words tumbled. 'The right mesh, we were out of it, it was on order, and the test was—Priya had it as must-run, it couldn't slip, and I had a roll of the other stuff right there and it looked the same and it's basically the same and I thought—' He stopped, the way he always stopped, right at the edge of the thing.

'You thought it was close enough,' Ellery said.

'I thought it was close enough.'

The room was quiet.

'How close is close enough,' Ellery said, 'for the thing that's supposed to save a man's life?'

Jonah didn't answer. He looked at his hands.

'I'm not asking to be cruel,' Ellery said. 'It's a real question and you're the only one who can answer it. You held two rolls of mesh. One was right and you didn't have it. One was wrong and you did. And you had forty minutes and a must-run flag and a boss you couldn't disappoint. So you made a judgment about how close was close enough.' She paused. 'You're a good technician. I've asked around. People say you're careful with your hands. The substitution probably looked fine on every test you could run. It conducts. It passes continuity. It just doesn't carry the load when the load is real.'

'It would've held for a normal run,' Jonah said. 'It probably would've held forever. It was just—it was just that one discharge. The big one. That's the only time it'd matter.'

'The big one is the only time any of it matters,' Ellery said. 'That's what the grounding mesh is for. Not the normal runs. The one that isn't normal. You substituted the part that only earns its keep on the worst day, and then the worst day came.'

He was very still.

'The report,' she said, 'will note that the grounding mesh repair used non-specification material. It will note that the correct material was unavailable and on order. It will note the time pressure under which the substitution was made.'

She stood.

'You didn't cut a corner because you were lazy,' she said. 'You cut it because you were scared, and out of stock, and out of time, and trying to make it work. That's almost worse. Lazy you could fix. This was you trying your hardest with the wrong roll of wire.'

She left the photograph on the table.
