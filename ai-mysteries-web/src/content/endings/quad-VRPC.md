No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had four findings and one chair she kept glancing at.

'Elias, Marcus, Jonah, Renée,' she said. 'The four of you. Not on the list: Priya.' She paused. 'Not because Priya did nothing. Because what Priya did is a different kind of thing, and I want to be precise. The four of you each made a hands-on failure—a calibration, an access override, a physical repair, a safety call. Priya set the conditions. She's upstream of all of you. So today I'm talking to the four who were downstream.'

The four were silent.

'Elias—you narrowed the threshold. Marcus—you overrode the door. Jonah—you skipped the check. Renée—you didn't halt in time.' She set them down. 'Four hands-on failures. And every one of them happened under a pressure that came from one place: the must-run schedule, the milestone, the deadline that wouldn't bend. You four are where the pressure became actions.'

'Then it's Priya you want,' Rourke said.

'It would be convenient,' Ellery said. 'Blame the boss, and the four of you are just leaves in her wind. But that's too easy, and it isn't true. Pressure doesn't pull a trigger. People do. Priya created an environment. The four of you decided what to do in it. Elias, you chose to defer rather than insist. Marcus, you chose to open the door. Jonah, you chose to skip the step. Renée, you chose to stay in your chair. The pressure was real. The choices were yours.'

The room was very quiet.

'Here's the trap of pointing upstream,' Ellery said. 'If all four of you blame Priya, then no one is responsible, because Priya didn't touch the suit, the door, the sensors, or the handle. The pressure has no fingerprints. The actions have four sets. You can't all be leaves. Somebody's hands were on each thing, and the somebodies are the four of you.'

'I deferred within the manual,' Varga said.

'I opened a door,' Rourke said.

'I ran out of time,' Jonah said.

'I was thirty feet away,' Renée said.

'Four hands, one wind,' Ellery said. 'The report will name the wind—Priya's schedule will be in it, plainly. But it will also name the four hands, because a death is made of hands, and these four were on it.'

She stood, and looked, once more, at the empty chair.

'It's tempting to put it all on the one who's not here,' she said. 'She'll get her own accounting. But the four of you decided, each in your moment, to go along. That's yours. You don't get to give it to her.'

She left the four findings on the table.
