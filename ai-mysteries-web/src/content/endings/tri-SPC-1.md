No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a compressed window, a skipped check, and an un-invoked halt.

'Priya—you cut the maintenance window to forty minutes and flagged the test must-run. Jonah—in that window you skipped the verification. Renée—you had the halt authority, and the doubt, and didn't use it.' She set them down. 'No time, no check, no stop.'

The three were silent.

'It runs downhill,' Ellery said. 'Priya's compressed, must-run schedule sets the slope. Jonah, under it, drops the check—there's no time and no permission to pause. Renée, you could have stood at the bottom and stopped it, and you didn't, because Priya's flag had taught you a halt wouldn't stick. The schedule pushed the technician and disarmed the safety officer in the same motion.'

'I set a schedule,' Priya said.

'I did what the window allowed,' Jonah said.

'I had been overruled before,' Renée said.

'There's a relationship map under this,' Ellery said. 'Priya, you protect Jonah, so Jonah won't fail your test—he cut the check rather than disappoint you. And your must-run flags, over years, are exactly what wore Renée down to where she stopped pulling the handle. So your one decision—the compressed must-run window—acted on both of them. It made Jonah skip and it made Renée stand still. Two failures, one cause, your cause.'

The room was very quiet.

'But it's not only you, Priya,' Ellery said. 'Jonah, you could have refused the impossible window—said it can't be done safely in forty minutes. Renée, you could have pulled the handle and made them overrule you out loud. Each of you had a moment to break the slope. You each deferred—to the schedule, to the loyalty, to the futility. Three deferrals, and the thing rolled all the way down.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the compressed must-run window, the skipped verification, and the un-invoked halt, and that the schedule pressure both caused the skipped check and contributed to the decision not to halt.'

She stood.

'You set the slope, you slipped on it, and you watched it roll,' she said. 'No time, no check, no stop. He was waiting at the bottom.'

She left the three records on the table.
