No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a suppressed alert and a bypass jumper.

'Marcus,' she said, 'you suppressed the Charge Cage proximity alert three weeks ago. Nuisance filtering. The room could no longer announce that someone had walked in.' She turned. 'Jonah, you left a bypass jumper across the interlock. The suit could no longer announce that it was about to discharge.' She set the small wire on the table next to the alert log. 'The room went silent. The suit went silent. Both of you, separately, turned off a voice.'

Jonah stared at the jumper. Rourke said nothing.

'Think about what those two systems are for,' Ellery said. 'The proximity alert is the building saying "a person is here." The interlock is the suit saying "I'm dangerous." Two warnings, from two directions, about the same situation: a man near a live suit. Either one, by itself, stops this. The building warns, someone pulls him out. The suit warns, it halts before the discharge.' She paused. 'Marcus silenced the building. Jonah silenced the suit. Neither of you knew the other had. So when the man stood near the live suit, the building didn't say he was there, and the suit didn't say it was dangerous, and the only two voices that could have spoken were both yours, and both off.'

'The jumper was an accident,' Jonah said, miserable. 'I forgot to pull it. I didn't—'

'The alert was deliberate,' Rourke said. 'But routine. It cried wolf constantly.'

'One forgotten, one deliberate,' Ellery said. 'It doesn't matter which. The result is the same: two silences, overlapping, exactly where a man stood. Jonah, your accident removed the suit's voice. Marcus, your routine removed the building's. And the overlap of an accident and a routine is a perfectly soundproof room, and he was standing in it.'

The room was very quiet.

'If the alert had been live,' Ellery said, 'someone gets the ping, the jumper doesn't matter—they haul him out before the discharge. If the jumper had been pulled, the suppressed alert doesn't matter—the interlock halts on its own. Each of your failures was survivable as long as the other's system worked. You took out each other's backups without ever meaning to, without ever speaking.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the suppressed proximity alert and the installed bypass jumper, that each independently silenced a warning, and that the death required both silences at once.'

She left the jumper where it was, next to the log.

'Two voices,' she said. 'The room's and the suit's. He'd have lived if either one of them could still speak. Between you, neither could.'

She left.
