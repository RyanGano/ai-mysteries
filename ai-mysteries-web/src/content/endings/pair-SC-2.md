No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a positioning note and a must-run flag.

'Priya,' she said, 'you flagged the test must-run, and you asked the safety officer to stand back from the halt station so the investor would have a clean view.' She turned. 'Renée, you complied. You stood thirty feet from the handle. When the fault came, you crossed the bay as fast as anyone could, and you reached the halt one minute too late.' She set the note down. 'You moved her away from the handle. She let herself be moved.'

The room was very still.

'I told her where to stand,' Priya said. 'I didn't tell the suit to fail. The positioning was an optics decision for a demonstration. It had nothing to do with—'

'It had everything to do with it,' Ellery said. 'The safety officer stands at the halt station for one reason: so she can reach the handle in time. That's the entire purpose of the position. You moved her off it for the look of the thing, and the look of the thing cost the thirty feet that cost the minute that cost the man.'

She turned to Renée.

'And you let yourself be moved,' she said. 'You knew where the safety officer is supposed to stand. You knew why. And you stood where you were told instead, because the fight over thirty feet didn't seem worth it that morning.'

'It was a demonstration,' Renée said quietly. 'Priya asked, and the request seemed reasonable, and I didn't want to be the difficult one over where I stood. I have spent so many years being the difficult one. I chose, that one morning, not to be.'

'That's the thing between you two,' Ellery said. 'Priya, you make the reasonable-sounding request—just stand back a little, it's only optics. And Renée, you, who could fight, decline to fight the small one. You save your battles for the big issues and treat your own position as a small one. But your position wasn't small. Your position was the thirty feet between a man and the handle. You let Priya negotiate away the one piece of ground that mattered, because she made it sound like nothing, and you wanted, for once, to not be the obstacle.'

'I didn't know the run would fail,' Priya said.

'And I didn't know thirty feet would be the difference,' Renée said.

'No,' Ellery said. 'Neither of you knew. But Priya, you have a gift for making safety feel like an obstacle to the moment, and Renée, you'd grown tired enough to let it. So when Priya said step back, you stepped back, and the step back was the whole margin. One of you asked for the thirty feet. One of you gave them. Together you put the safety officer exactly far enough from the handle to be too late.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the request to reposition the safety officer for the demonstration, the safety officer's compliance, and that the resulting distance contributed to the one-minute delay in the halt.'

She stood.

'Thirty feet,' she said. 'One of you asked, one of you yielded, and the man died in the seconds it took to cross them.'

She left the positioning note and the flag on the table.
