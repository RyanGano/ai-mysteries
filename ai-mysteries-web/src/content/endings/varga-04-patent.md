No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a patent filing.

'Provisional application,' she said. 'The adaptive load-balancing method. Filed eighteen months ago.' She slid it across the table. 'Inventor listed: Michael Holloway. Sole.'

Elias Varga did not pick it up.

'The method is yours,' Ellery said. 'I've read your notebooks. I've read the early drafts. The architecture is your handwriting from end to end. Michael's name is on it because Michael ran the room when it was filed.'

'Michael ran the rooms,' Varga said. 'That was Michael's contribution to everything. Standing in rooms.'

'There's a correction window,' she said. 'A way to amend inventorship. There was an error in the original submission—a real one, this time, a clerical one—and amending it would have reopened the question of who actually invented the method.' She watched him. 'You found the error.'

'I find errors. It's a great deal of what I do.'

'You found it,' she said, 'and you didn't file the correction. You let the window close. The patent stayed in Michael's name.'

Varga was very still.

'I don't think you did it to protect Michael,' Ellery said. 'I think you did it because a corrected patent would have made the method yours, officially, on the record, in the same month it killed someone. And you couldn't—' she paused. 'You couldn't be the named inventor of the thing that failed. So you let the lie stand. You let it stay his.'

'That's a story,' Varga said. 'You're telling yourself a story about a man's motives from a filing date.'

'I am,' Ellery agreed. 'It's a good story. It explains why a man who has corrected every error he's ever encountered chose to leave this one alone.' She tapped the document. 'You'd rather it was his failure than your invention.'

He said nothing for a long time.

'The report,' she said, 'will note that the method underlying the incident was attributed to Michael Holloway. It will note that the attribution was known to be incorrect. It will note that the person who knew chose not to correct it.'

She gathered her things.

'You wanted credit your whole career,' she said. 'And when it finally mattered, you gave it away. To a dead man. So it wouldn't be yours.'

She left the filing on the table.

His name was nowhere on it.
