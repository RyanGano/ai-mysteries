No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a thread of replies.

'Renée Calder raised a concern,' she said. 'In writing. About the interlock, ten days before the incident. She used the word "hazard." She asked for the test program to pause.' She looked at Priya Shah. 'You replied. You changed the word.'

'I reframed it,' Priya said. 'Renée catastrophizes. It's her function and she's good at it, but every issue is a hazard to Renée, every delay is a near-disaster. If you accept her framing on everything, you halt forever. Part of my job is to translate her language into terms the business can actually weigh.'

'You translated "hazard" into "risk appetite,"' Ellery said. She read from the screen. '"This falls within our current risk appetite. Recommend we proceed and revisit post-milestone."' She looked up. 'You took a safety word and turned it into a finance word.'

'Those aren't opposites,' Priya said. 'Risk appetite is how a serious organization talks about hazard. It means we acknowledge it and we've decided what level we'll accept. That's more honest than pretending we can eliminate all risk. Renée wants zero. Zero isn't a real number in this business.'

'It's a different number for the person inside the suit,' Ellery said.

Priya was quiet.

'Here's what the reframe does,' Ellery said. 'When Renée says "hazard," someone has to respond to a hazard. There are procedures. Halts. Escalations. When you say "risk appetite," it becomes a number on a chart that's already been decided. The hazard doesn't go away. It just stops being something anyone has to act on. You didn't refute her. You relabeled her into silence.'

'I made a decision and I documented my reasoning,' Priya said. 'That's the opposite of silence. It's accountability. My name is right there on the call.'

'Your name is on the call,' Ellery agreed. 'And the call was to take a warning and reclassify it as an accepted cost. "Revisit post-milestone." The milestone was the test. He died at the milestone. There was no post.'

The room was very still.

'You're extraordinary at this,' Ellery said. 'I mean that. I've watched you do it across a dozen documents. Someone hands you a problem and you hand back a tradeoff. A hazard becomes an appetite. A failure becomes a risk. A death becomes—' she stopped. 'Be careful what you call it in the next few weeks. People are listening for it now.'

'The report,' she said, 'will include the original concern and your reply. Both words. "Hazard" and "risk appetite." Side by side. People can decide which one the morning proved correct.'

She stood.

'You speak as if outcomes justify the decision,' she said. 'Backward. The decision was right because it worked out. But this one didn't work out. So now we get to see what your language was actually holding up the whole time.'

She left the thread on the screen.
