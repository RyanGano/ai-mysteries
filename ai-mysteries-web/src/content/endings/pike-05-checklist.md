No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a checklist. Every box was ticked.

'Twenty-two items,' she said. 'Pre-test verification. Every one initialed. JP. JP. JP. All the way down.' She looked at Jonah Pike. 'How long does it take to actually do all twenty-two?'

'About—' Jonah started, and then he saw where it was going, and his face closed.

'The timestamps say you completed the sheet in nine minutes,' Ellery said. 'Item one to item twenty-two. Nine minutes. I had Renée walk me through what the items are. Some of them take longer than nine minutes by themselves.'

Jonah didn't say anything.

'You signed for things you didn't do,' Ellery said. Gently. 'Didn't you.'

'I did most of them,' he said. Very quiet. 'I did the ones that mattered. I know which ones matter. Some of them are—half of them are stuff that's fine every single time. The torque on a panel you didn't open. The seal on a port nobody touched. You sign those because they're always fine. Everybody does it. You can't actually do all twenty-two on every run, nobody does, there isn't time—'

'There's never time,' Ellery said. 'I've heard that from everyone in this building.'

'There's never time,' Jonah agreed, miserable.

The room was silent.

'Here's the thing about a checklist,' Ellery said. 'It's not for you. You know what you did. The checklist is for the next person. It's a promise to whoever reads it that these twenty-two things are true. Michael Holloway didn't check the suit himself. He trusted the sheet. The sheet said all twenty-two were verified. He put on a suit that a piece of paper told him was safe.'

'I didn't lie about the dangerous one,' Jonah said. 'The one that—I checked that one. I swear I checked the one that—' and then he stopped, because he didn't actually know which one had failed, and they both understood it at the same moment.

'You don't know which one mattered,' Ellery said softly. 'That's the trap. You signed all twenty-two so you wouldn't have to know. If you'd only checked the ones you were sure mattered, you'd at least know which gamble you took. But you ticked every box, so now any of them could be the one. You hid the failure from yourself in a row of identical initials.'

Jonah put his face in his hands.

'It's happened before,' Ellery said. 'Hasn't it. The signing-ahead. It's a habit, not a one-time thing.'

He didn't deny it. The not-denying was the answer.

'The report,' she said, 'will note that the pre-test verification sheet was completed in a timeframe inconsistent with performing the listed checks. It will note that all items were initialed.'

She stood.

'You've gotten away with it a hundred times,' she said. 'That's what habits are. A hundred times the box was a lie and it didn't matter because the thing was fine anyway.' She paused. 'And then once it wasn't fine, and the lie was already signed.'

She left the checklist on the table, twenty-two perfect ticks.
