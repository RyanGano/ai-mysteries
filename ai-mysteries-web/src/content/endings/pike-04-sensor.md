No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had two sensor packs in evidence bags.

'These are interchangeable,' she said. 'Same connector. Same housing. You can fit either one into the suit and it'll seat perfectly.' She set them side by side. 'They're calibrated for different units. This one's for Suit Three. This one's for Suit Seven. Michael Holloway was wearing Suit Seven.'

Jonah Pike looked at the bags. His face went through something.

'They got mixed up,' he said. 'In the bin. They were both in the bin on the bench. I grabbed the—' He stopped.

'You grabbed the wrong one,' Ellery said.

'They're not labeled,' Jonah said, fast now. 'I mean they are, there's a little number, but it's tiny, it's etched on the back where you can't see it when it's in the bin, and I was going so fast, and they're identical, you can't tell them apart by looking—' He ran out of breath.

'So Suit Seven ran on Suit Three's calibration,' Ellery said. 'Every reading off by the difference between two units. The power draw, the contact pressure, the temperature—all of it slightly wrong. Wrong in a way that looked completely normal, because the numbers were real numbers, just from the wrong suit.'

'It would've flagged,' Jonah said, almost pleading. 'You'd think it would've flagged. Wrong calibration, the system should catch that, it should know—'

'It didn't know,' Ellery said. 'The pack doesn't announce which suit it's for. It just reports numbers. The system trusted the numbers. The numbers were honest. They were just answering for the wrong body.'

The room was very quiet.

'I checked it,' Jonah said. He was looking at the table. 'I want you to know I checked it. I seated it and I ran the self-test and it passed. It passed because a sensor pack passes its self-test no matter which suit it thinks it's in. The self-test doesn't know either.' He looked up, and there was something desperate in it. 'How was I supposed to catch it if even the suit couldn't?'

'You were supposed to read the number on the back,' Ellery said. Not unkindly. 'The tiny one. In the bin. Before you grabbed it. That's the whole job, that one second of reading the number. And you didn't have the second, because they took it from you. But it was still your hand on the pack.'

He nodded slowly. He didn't argue.

'The report,' she said, 'will note that the sensor pack installed in Suit Seven was calibrated for a different unit. It will note that the packs are physically interchangeable and minimally marked. It will note that the substitution was undetectable to the suit's own self-test.'

She gathered the bags.

'You're not a careless person,' she said. 'I've decided that about you. You're a careful person who was put in a careless situation and asked to be perfect in it. The number was a millimeter high and the light was bad and the clock was running. And a millimeter was enough.'

She left.
