No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had an org chart.

'You're next in line,' she said. 'If Michael steps back, you run this place. Everyone knows it. You know it.' She looked at Priya Shah. 'And the week before the incident, Michael was talking about not stepping back. About taking the reins again. The funding scare had him reconsidering the succession he'd promised you.'

'Founders second-guess succession constantly,' Priya said. 'It's noise. You learn to ignore it. I had a transition memo, I had board support, I had a timeline. One nervous founder talking at a dinner doesn't undo any of that.'

'It undoes the timeline,' Ellery said. 'Your timeline. The one where you take over clean, on schedule, with the milestone hit and the funding secured. Michael coming back fast scrambles all of it. Suddenly you're not the heir, you're the deputy again. Indefinitely.'

'You're describing ordinary corporate friction,' Priya said. 'It's not a motive for anything. People work under bosses they'd like to replace every day. They don't—' she stopped.

'They don't what?' Ellery said.

Priya didn't finish it.

'I'm not going to say the word you're not saying either,' Ellery said. 'I have no evidence you planned his death. None. What I have is this: you wanted him gone, professionally, and you said so, in writing, to two board members. And on the morning a test killed him, every operational lever you controlled was set to make that test run. The schedule. The override. The pressure on the floor.' She paused. 'You didn't have to plan it. You only had to keep pushing, and not look too hard at what you were pushing toward.'

The room was silent.

'That's the thing about ambition like yours,' Ellery said. 'It doesn't need a plan. It just needs you to keep your eyes on the milestone and not on the man standing between you and it. You weren't aiming at Michael. You were aiming past him. He was just in the way of where you were looking.'

'I wanted the company,' Priya said. Her voice was very level. 'I won't pretend I didn't. I would have run it better than he did. That's not a confession. That's an assessment.'

'I know,' Ellery said. 'You speak in outcomes. The outcome is that you got the thing you wanted, on a morning you'd set up to get a different thing, and the price was a man you'd told two people you wanted out of your way.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the succession tension, documented in your own correspondence. It will note your operational decisions on the day. It will place them next to each other. That's all. It won't draw the conclusion. It won't have to.'

She stood.

'You're going to get the company,' she said. 'That's almost certainly true now. I just wanted you to sit in this room once, with all of it laid out, and feel how you got it.'

She left the org chart on the table, your name at the top.
