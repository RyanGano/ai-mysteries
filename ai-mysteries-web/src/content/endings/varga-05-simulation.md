No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a simulation log.

'You ran this on your own machine,' she said. 'Not the lab cluster. Your personal workstation, after hours. Forty-one times.' She read from the timestamp column. 'The last run was eleven days before the incident.'

Elias Varga's expression did not change.

'It's a Monte Carlo,' she said. 'A failure model. You were sweeping the suit's power profile against the interlock thresholds and looking at where it breaks.' She looked up. 'In thirty-eight of the forty-one runs, it didn't break. In three of them, it did. The three were edge cases. Rare. Within the tail of the distribution.'

'Which is precisely why one runs a simulation,' Varga said. 'To characterize the tail. That is the entire purpose of the exercise.'

'And what do you do,' Ellery said, 'when the exercise tells you the tail includes a fatality?'

He didn't answer.

'You characterize it,' she said. 'You wrote a paragraph. I've read it. It's careful, it's precise, it concludes that the failure mode is "low-probability but non-zero and merits further investigation." And then—' she turned the page. 'And then nothing. You didn't send it to anyone. You didn't open a safety ticket. You filed it in a folder called "drafts."'

'It was preliminary,' Varga said. 'Preliminary findings are not escalated. That's not how the process works. You don't raise an alarm on a model you haven't validated. You'd be crying wolf on every simulation that ever produced a bad sample.'

'Eleven days,' Ellery said.

'Strictly speaking, validation takes—'

'Eleven days, Elias.'

The room was silent.

'You knew,' she said quietly. 'Not for certain. You're never certain—that's your defense and it's a real one. But you knew enough to run it forty-one times. You knew enough to write the paragraph. And then you decided that "non-zero" was the same thing as "not yet."'

'It was within the noise,' he said. 'It was within tolerance.'

'It was in the folder,' she said.

She closed the log.

'The report will note that a predictive model of the failure existed. It will note its author. It will note its filing location and its access timestamps.' She paused. 'It will note that no one read it until I did.'

She stood.

'You answered the question,' she said. 'You just answered it where no one could hear you. I think that's the only way you could bear to answer it at all.'

She left the log open on the desk, on the three runs that broke.
