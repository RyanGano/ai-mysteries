No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had a near-miss report and a reused part.

'Three weeks before the incident,' she said, 'Suit Seven threw the same fault in an empty bay. Priya, that near-miss landed on your desk and you downgraded it to "monitor."' She turned. 'Jonah, the part that failed that morning was a single-use gasket you'd reused four times—pulled from a drawer, to save the budget Priya was always pushing.' She set the records down. 'A warning you waved off. A corner he cut to please you. The same fault, twice.'

'A single unmanned event isn't a pattern,' Priya said.

'I was trying to save money,' Jonah said. 'The parts looked fine.'

'Let me show you how these two things are actually one thing,' Ellery said. 'Priya, you run this place on cost discipline and schedule. You praise thrift. You praise people who make the numbers work. Jonah hears that—Jonah, who'd do anything for your approval—and Jonah reuses a part to save money, because saving money is how you earn a smile from Priya.' She paused. 'Then the reused part throws a warning, three weeks early, in the empty bay. And it lands on your desk, Priya, and you downgrade it, because acting on it would cost time and money—the very things you'd taught Jonah to protect.'

The room was quiet.

'Do you see the loop?' Ellery said. 'Your priorities, Priya, taught Jonah to cut the corner. The corner threw a warning. Your priorities, Priya, taught you to dismiss the warning. The same values that created the fault disposed of the evidence of the fault. Jonah supplied the danger because he wanted to please you. You ignored the danger because acknowledging it would have cost what you'd both been trained to never spend.'

'You're blaming me for his shortcut and his shortcut on me,' Priya said. 'That's circular.'

'It is circular,' Ellery agreed. 'That's exactly what it is. A loop. Jonah cuts to save, the saving causes a fault, you dismiss the fault to keep saving, the fault comes back bigger. Round and round, three weeks, until a man was inside it. Neither of you sits at a single guilty point. You sit on a circle, passing the same value back and forth—do it cheaper, do it faster—until it killed someone.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the reused single-use part, the downgraded near-miss with the matching fault signature, and the shared operating priorities that produced both.'

She stood.

'You make a good team,' she said, and it wasn't a compliment. 'You really do. She sets the values, you embody them, she protects you for it. It's efficient. It's loyal. And it ran in a circle until a man fell into the middle of it.'

She left the near-miss and the gasket on the table.
