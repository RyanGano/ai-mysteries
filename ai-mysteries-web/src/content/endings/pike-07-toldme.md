No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery sat down across from Jonah Pike and didn't put anything on the table.

'I know how the suit was misconfigured,' she said. 'I know the step that got skipped, and I know your hands skipped it. That part's settled.' She watched him. 'What I want to know is whose voice was in your head when you did it.'

Jonah looked at her, then away.

'Somebody told you it was fine,' Ellery said. 'Didn't they. You weren't sure, and you asked, or you almost asked, and somebody who outranks you told you not to worry about it. And you did what they said, because that's what you do. You look to other people before you speak. I've watched you do it in this room.'

'Nobody told me to do anything wrong,' Jonah said, automatically.

'I didn't say wrong,' Ellery said. 'I said somebody told you it was fine. Those aren't the same. Somebody you trust looked at the same shortcut you were worried about and said, that's fine, Jonah, ship it. And the worry went away, because it wasn't your worry to carry anymore. It was theirs now. You'd handed it up.'

Jonah's jaw worked.

'Here's the trouble,' Ellery said. 'You handed up the worry. But you didn't hand up the wrench. Your hands still did it. So when I go looking for who's responsible, the trail runs through you, because you're the one who touched the suit. The person who told you it was fine—their fingerprints aren't on anything. They just said a word. Words don't show up in the wreckage. Hands do.'

The room was quiet.

'Who was it, Jonah?'

He didn't answer. He looked at the floor.

'You're protecting them,' Ellery said. 'Of course you are. They protect you. That's the deal. They keep you employed, they smooth things over, and in exchange you carry the weight when it comes down. You're loyal. It's one of the good things about you, and it's the thing that's going to bury you, because loyalty going up the ladder never comes back down it.'

'They didn't mean for anybody to get hurt,' Jonah said, finally. It wasn't a name. But it was close to one.

'I don't think anybody meant for anybody to get hurt,' Ellery said. 'That's not what I'm trying to establish. I'm trying to establish that you were told it was fine, by someone, and you believed them, and you were wrong to. Not because they lied. Because it was never their hands. It was always going to be yours.'

'The report,' she said, 'will note the configuration error and your role in it. It will note your account that you sought and received assurance before proceeding. If you give me the name, the report notes that too.'

She stood. She waited a moment. He didn't give it.

'You'll tell me eventually,' she said. 'Not today. But someday it's going to cost you more to keep it than to say it. I'll be around.'

She left.

He stayed at the table a long time, looking at his hands, the ones that had done it on someone else's word.
