No one asked why they were there this time.
The meeting had been called without an agenda, without a subject line, without the small procedural courtesies that usually softened bad news. That alone told them this wasn't another reconstruction.

Detective Mara Ellery had two incident summaries.

'SAM generates these,' she said. 'After any event, it compiles a summary from the logs. Neutral. Automatic.' She laid down the first. 'This is the summary SAM generated at 10:00. And this—' she laid down the second, 'is the summary that was circulated to the board at noon. They have the same header. They are not the same document.'

Priya Shah looked at them.

'Summaries get edited for clarity,' she said. 'A raw machine output isn't a board document. Someone has to make it readable. That's not tampering, that's communication.'

'The raw version mentions the override,' Ellery said. 'The anomalous reading. The schedule pressure. Renée's unanswered memo. The board version mentions a "component anomaly under investigation."' She looked up. 'You didn't make it readable. You made it small.'

'I summarized a summary,' Priya said. 'That's what an executive does with information before it goes up. You distill. You don't drown the board in raw telemetry they can't interpret. Every one of those details has an innocent technical explanation, and including all of them would have created a misleading impression of chaos.'

'A misleading impression,' Ellery said. 'Of chaos. As opposed to the accurate impression.'

Priya was quiet.

'Here's what interests me,' Ellery said. 'You have the authority to shape what SAM surfaces. You influence what it prioritizes, what it summarizes, what rises and what stays buried. That's a quiet kind of power. SAM answers honestly—but you get to choose which honest answer the board hears.' She paused. 'You chose the one with the fewest threads to pull.'

'I chose the one that wouldn't trigger a panic that helps no one,' Priya said. 'The man was already dead. Drowning the board in alarm wouldn't bring him back. It would just end the program and put a hundred people out of work over an incident that was, by every individual measure, within parameters.'

'Within tolerance,' Ellery said.

'Within tolerance,' Priya said.

The room was very still.

'You didn't lie to the board,' Ellery said. 'I'll give you that. Every word in your version is true. You just removed the words that would have made them ask the next question. That's your particular gift. You don't falsify. You curate.'

'The report,' she said, 'will include both summaries. The one SAM generated and the one the board received. It will note the differences. It will let people read the gap.'

She stood.

'You manage the narrative,' she said. 'You've done it brilliantly your whole career. But there's a version of events SAM wrote down before you got to it, and I have it, and it's the truest thing in this building, because it's the only account no one was managing.'

She left both summaries on the table.
